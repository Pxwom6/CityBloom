# Decisions

One line each: what was decided and why. Newest at the bottom of each section.

## M0
- Working title **Citybloom** (`GAME_TITLE` in `src/config.ts`): original, warm, no genre trademark.
- TypeScript 6.0 rather than 7.0: typescript-eslint supports TS < 6.1 only.
- @playwright/test pinned to 1.56.x to match the pre-installed Chromium 141 (build 1194) in the cloud VM.
- Compressed calendar: one day/night cycle is one calendar month (1440 one-minute ticks), so lighting, rush hours and the monthly budget share one clock and 20 game years stay playable.
- Speeds 1×/2×/3× = 8/16/24 ticks per second (a month takes 3 / 1.5 / 1 minute).
- Preact for the UI: tiny, JSX, hooks; panels like the budget and inspector would be painful in raw DOM.
- Buildings are chunk-merged rather than instanced, because procedural variety makes too many distinct models for instancing to pay off.
- Traffic is an aggregated assignment that also performs job/shop matching, so employment depends on the real road network.

## M1
- Roads are quadratic Béziers; free-form strokes are fitted with a chain of ~48 m quadratics.
- Roads drape over the terrain (no terrain flattening); max grade 12 % over 16 m, min curve radius 18 m, min junction angle 28°.
- Crossing an existing road splits both at the crossing; ending on a road makes a T-junction. Crossing the regional highway is not allowed.
- Water blocks roads until bridges arrive in M6.
- Zone cells are 8 m squares, 4 rows deep on both sides of every road; overlaps resolve by priority (occupied, then lower row, older road, lower column). Cells that become invalid lose their zoning.
- Zone painting only changes empty cells: existing buildings keep their cells until bulldozed, so a stray brush stroke never destroys a neighbourhood.
- A brush drag is one undo step (commands carry a stroke id and the sim merges them).
- Bulldozing a road refunds 25 %; undo refunds 100 % and re-merges roads it split. Undo covers placements only (spec).
- Removing a road removes its zone blocks; buildings on them are demolished (shown in the bulldoze preview from M2).
- Snapping (nodes, roads, 45° steps relative to world axes and existing roads, optional 8 m grid) happens on the client for responsiveness; the sim re-validates every command.
- Keyboard: T roads, Z/X/C/V zones (R/C/I/dezone), B bulldoze, H or Esc select, Tab road mode, G grid, [ ] brush size, Ctrl+Z or U undo. WASD/QE/RF/+− stay on the camera.
- Save format stays at version 1 until M2 introduces real save/load; from then on every format change bumps the version with a migration.

## M2
- Buildings are archetypes by zone × density × wealth × level (`src/data/buildings.ts`); density is set by the road type and population unlocks (medium at 800, high at 5,000), wealth by land value.
- If a bigger footprint doesn't fit when upgrading, the building rebuilds in place on its lot (capacity scales with lot area), so packed streets still upgrade.
- Employment and shopping come from one nearest-first matcher over the road graph (DESIGN §3.8), so jobs need a road connection. Residents from outside the city don't commute in (no regional commuters) to keep the loop readable.
- New residents aren't counted as unemployed until the next matching round (avoids a move-in/move-out flicker).
- Upgrades need mood ≥ 0.62, occupancy ≥ 88 % for 3 hours and positive demand for that zone. 0.62 is reachable before services exist; revisit once services add positive factors (M5/M12).
- High wealth can't move in until services exist (M5); M2 caps wealth at medium.
- Industry "wealth" is the industry tier (heavy, manufacturing, high-tech); M2 grows heavy industry only (education drives tiers in M7).
- Growth needs the lot's road to be connected to the highway; cut-off buildings gain distress twice as fast and are abandoned within about a day.
- Demolishing zoned buildings is free (no cost, no refund).
- Loading a save reloads the page into `?load=<slot>`; this keeps the renderer's setup path single and robust.
- Saves are gzip-compressed JSON in IndexedDB; exports use the same bytes with a `.citybloom` extension.

## M3
- Money is integer dollars. Hourly accruals carry their fractional remainder per ledger category, so `treasury = month-start treasury + Σ ledger lines` holds exactly every tick (checked in test mode).
- A month is one day/night cycle, so monthly rates accrue in 24 hourly steps; the budget closes at midnight and keeps 24 months of history.
- Loans are annuities paid hourly (interest first); up to 3 at once; early repayment settles the balance. Bigger loans unlock with population.
- Bankruptcy: the treasury may be negative for 48 hours (two months); then the city is bankrupt, the sim stops and every command is refused. Sandbox never goes bankrupt.
- Low-money warning when the monthly net is negative and the treasury covers less than three months of it.
- Tax changes affect demand (average rate per zone), spawn chance per wealth level, and each building's mood (weighted by wealth sensitivity).
- Department funding scales upkeep linearly; effectiveness follows `f ≤ 1 ? f : 1 + ½(f − 1)`. Road maintenance funding only scales cost until traffic (M6) uses it for road wear.
- Policies are listed under Money in the spec but built in M10 with progression; the ledger already has a `policies` line.
- Budget charts use two single-axis charts (treasury line, net-income bars in the diverging blue/red poles), never a dual axis.
- Save format v2 adds the economy; `migrations[1]` upgrades v1 saves with default taxes and funding (tested).

## M4
- Service vehicles move at a visual speed (0.2 m per tick per m/s of road speed), not at real speed in game time; incident timings are tuned in ticks to match (DESIGN §3.7).
- Civic buildings are placed beside a road, facing it, sliding along it with the cursor; the sim validates road frontage, water, slope, overlaps, unlocks and money. Zoned buildings in the way are demolished (shown in the preview).
- Utilities go to consumers in order of road distance from the nearest producer, per connected network; with too little capacity the far end goes dark first.
- Consumption follows capacity (not occupancy) so supply needs don't swing with move-ins.
- Businesses close after 12 hours without power or water and reopen when both return; homes don't close but their mood drops (-28 % each for power and water, -15 % for sewage), which leads to abandonment within about two days.
- A pump draws polluted water when ground pollution under it exceeds 0.25; its share of the network's water is "polluted" for every building on that network (sickness arrives in M7).
- Garbage accumulates per building; trucks go to the fullest buildings within reach and also empty neighbours within 48 m. Landfills fill up; recycling earns trade revenue; incinerators turn yesterday's burn into power.
- Bulldozing a civic building refunds 25 %; undo refunds 100 %.
- Data maps mute the scene and paint values with the reference data-viz ramps: diverging red–grey–blue for supply (bad→good), sequential blue for amounts. A legend names both ends.
- Problem icons are one Points draw call with a canvas-drawn atlas; one icon per building, most urgent first.

## M5
- Coverage is sampled every 24 m along each road (not only at junctions), so it fades smoothly along long streets; buildings read it at their frontage and the data maps tint the roads themselves.
- Service ranges are travel seconds at free-flow speed: a fire station fully covers ~300 m of street and fades out by ~600 m, so a mid-sized town needs several stations. Parks reach only a block or two.
- The coverage table is cached and dropped whenever roads, service buildings or funding change, keeping it a pure function of saved state (exact reloads mid-hour).
- Buildings without power or water (below half) now gain distress whatever their mood: services had made towns happy enough to shrug off a blackout, which isn't believable.
- In-place upgrades don't use up new-building construction slots; otherwise a happy town stopped spreading while it upgraded.
- Garbage trucks carry 90 units (was 60): one landfill now serves about 2,500 residents.
- Fire engines lower intensity by 0.012 per tick at the scene, so a fire caught early is out in about an hour; unanswered fires burn for ~16 hours before collapsing into rubble, which clears after 36 hours.
- Crimes and emergencies are incidents with a response window (7 h and 10 h); only unanswered ones raise the crime map or cost a life, so police and ambulances matter through response time.
- Health capacity (beds) is shown only once sickness exists (M7); in M5 clinics and hospitals provide coverage and ambulances.
- Wealth level 2 needs fire, police and health coverage ≥ 0.4 at the lot; wealthier homes weigh missing services 1.5× (low wealth 0.6×).
- The inspector lists up to three "would help" suggestions drawn from a building's worst mood factors.
- A debug/test `ignite` cheat starts a fire in a building; M9's disasters menu builds on it.
- Toasts move left of the inspector while it's open so they never cover it.
- Data maps are lit as midday whatever the clock, so they stay readable at night.

## M6
- Traffic volumes are part of the saved state (daily PCU per segment), updated every matcher round by successive averages with step 0.2: 0.35 flip-flopped between routes; 0.2 settles within a game day.
- The day is compressed: 25 % of a day's traffic falls in the rush hour, so small towns flow and towns of a few thousand jam at bottlenecks.
- BPR alone made a jammed 120 m link cost seconds; an oversaturated road also adds the average queue wait (up to 10 minutes × (1 − c/v)), which is what makes a bottleneck hurt.
- Coverage maps stay on free-flow times (stable, cacheable); real responses are slowed by traffic via the vehicles' congested speeds.
- Visible traffic is client-only and non-deterministic: it samples real sim trips but never feeds back. Trip samples aren't saved; after loading, cars reappear at the next assignment round (≤ 2 game hours).
- Freight imports and exports always succeed through a connected highway; goods shortages don't yet affect shops (possible M10 trade specialisation hook).
- Road upgrades cost the difference between the two road types (downgrades are free, no refund) and keep buildings by keeping cell indices; buildings are lost only where the new width leaves no room.
- Bridges need at least a street, span at most 360 m and need ~68 m of dry land at each end for ramps; roads can't meet on a bridge.
- Buses follow SimCity 2013: a depot plus stops, no hand-drawn lines. Each depot runs one loop through the stops it reaches first, visiting them nearest-first. Commuters choose by door-to-door time with a 300 s car-hassle allowance; shopping trips stay by car.
- Road unlocks (boulevards at 20k) are now enforced by the sim as well as the UI.

## M7
- The wind is a pure function of the seed and the tick (a prevailing direction with a seasonal wobble), so it needs no saved state and the renderer's smoke drifts the same way as the sim's pollution.
- Sickness is aggregated per home as a fractional count (no individual residents); deaths come off the population as whole residents, the fraction by chance.
- Hospital and clinic "beds" use the M5 capacity field; beds go to the nearest sick first, like school seats.
- Education is an average level per home rather than per-resident degrees; the shares at each level are read off the average. This keeps saves small and still lets schooling visibly move industry up the tiers.
- Industry retools to the tier the workforce supports without needing the usual upgrade conditions (2 % chance per building per hour): tiers track education, not mood.
- Trees and parks absorb air pollution; there's no tree-planting tool (SimCity 2013 had none either) — parks are the player's lever.
- Unprotected fires spread much less than in M5 (spread chance 0.06 → 0.02 per 30 ticks, collapse after 450 ticks instead of 700): the e2e screenshots showed whole streets burning in towns without a fire station.
- The air pollution map shows values ×2.5 so that the thin but harmful downwind plume (0.05–0.2) is visible.

## M8
- Advisors are pure queries over the sim state every 2 s rather than saved state: nothing to migrate, and they can never disagree with the data maps they point at.
- Only the first new urgent advice of a refresh pops up as a toast; the rest go straight to the notification log. The first e2e screenshot showed three red toasts about power, water and sewage at once.
- Resident thoughts are chosen by hashing building ids with the game hour, not with the sim RNG, so asking for them can't change the simulation.
- Street names live only on the client (derived from segment ids); a save doesn't need them and they come out the same on every load. *Superseded by P10: street names are saved per segment (save v24).*
- Audio is not positional: at city scale the mix already follows the view, and panning individual sources would cost more than it adds.
- Pedestrians walk the sim's sampled short trips, but those trips are still counted as car trips on the roads; the walkers are a visible sample, not a separate travel mode. A walking mode share is a possible M12 refinement.
- Tilt-shift works on the finished frame (copy + two blur passes) instead of an EffectComposer chain, so the tone-mapped look and the custom shaders (sky, water, lamp pools, smoke) stay identical with it on or off. It's off by default.
- Player settings (volumes, mute, tilt-shift) live in localStorage, per device rather than per city; M11 adds the rest to the same record.
- Street-lamp light is faked with additive quads (with polygon offset so they survive at distance) rather than real lights, which would multiply the lighting cost per lamp.
- The variety pass adds about 45 % more triangles to a low-density home (93 → 139 on average) for gardens, trees and cars; mid and high density are almost unchanged, and those dominate big cities.

## M9
- Disasters are saved state, but their motion (tornado position, flood level, meteor fall) is a pure function of their parameters and the tick, so the client animates them smoothly and a save/load mid-disaster carries on identically.
- Floods set their peak from the local shoreline (1.8 m above the typical land near the water) rather than a fixed height: the maps' banks range from ~3 m to ~11 m, and a fixed level either did nothing or drowned everything.
- Closed roads leave the routing graph, but "linked to the highway" uses the network as built: otherwise a day's repairs marked whole streets as cut off, doubled their distress and emptied them.
- Disaster repairs take 6–24 h for roads and 8–24 h for civic buildings (the first draft had up to 72 h): longer outages pushed whole towns past the 48-hour abandonment threshold and they never recovered.
- Abandoned buildings nobody moves back into now crumble after four days. Without this, a town that lost its businesses in a disaster stayed scarred for good, since empty shops can't pass the reoccupy test with no customers around.
- Earthquake magnitudes are skewed towards the low end (5.6 + 1.8·u²): a magnitude-7 quake in the middle of a small town destroys about a third of it, which is right for a rare big one but too much as the average.
- A direct meteor hit destroys a civic building outright (the player rebuilds it); other disasters only knock civic buildings offline for repairs.
- The disasters menu works whether or not random disasters are on: the setting only stops them striking by themselves.
- Disaster collapses are their own event ('collapsed', logged but not toasted); the report when a disaster is over sums up the damage instead of a toast per building.

## M10
- Unlocks go by the highest population ever reached, not the current one: losing residents to a disaster or a bad budget shouldn't take away buildings the player has already used.
- Every unlock threshold sits exactly on a named milestone (nine, from Hamlet to Metropolis, never more than 2.5× apart), so each one arrives as a batch with a celebration instead of things trickling in unannounced. Existing thresholds moved to the nearest milestone (e.g. gas power 1,200 → 2,000, hospital 4,000 → 5,000, university 15,000 → 20,000).
- The unlock-all cheat opens up buildings, roads, policies and modules but not zone densities, which follow the city's own growth; sandbox mode unlocks both. Tests rely on the cheat to place services without changing how towns grow.
- Each policy has exactly one effect in one system, sized to be clearly measurable (−25 % to −50 %), so tests can check it and players can see it on the relevant data map or ledger line.
- Free buses have no fares to remove (the game never charged fares), so they act on the mode choice: the bus counts as five minutes quicker.
- Modules are added from the building's inspector, once each, and appear as a small annex in a back corner of the lot rather than extending the footprint, which would have to re-check roads and neighbours.
- Specialisation buildings are civic buildings with extra fields (tourism, resource, freight, research), so placement, funding, upkeep, damage, disasters and the inspector all work for them unchanged. Two new budget departments (Tourism; Trade and research) scale them.
- Visitors don't drive through the traffic model yet (they're counted, spend and shop); tourist traffic is a possible M12 addition.
- Landmarks are unique; hotels, mines, wells and freight terminals aren't (a second terminal adds half again, a third nothing).
- Achievements are checked in the sim (deterministic, saved with the city) and are off in sandbox mode.
- Growth all the way to 100,000 residents is exercised in M12's large-city benchmark; M10 checks the unlock table itself (every milestone unlocks something, nothing unlocks between them).

## M11
- Starting, loading or quitting a city reloads the page (with `?new=1&…` or `?load=<slot>`), instead of tearing down and rebuilding the worker, renderer and mirror in place: it's the simplest way to guarantee a clean slate every time. The URL is reset to `/` after boot so a browser reload lands on the main menu, not on a fresh copy of the city.
- The main menu is drawn over a real, paused map (the default seed) with the camera slowly circling, rather than a static picture, so the first thing a player sees is the game's own look.
- Continue opens the newest save of any kind (autosave, quick or named). Quitting to the menu autosaves first, so Continue always resumes exactly where the player left.
- Escape opens the pause menu only when there's nothing else for it to do (cancel a drag, leave a tool, deselect, close a panel), as in most PC games. Any menu over a city pauses it and restores the previous speed on close.
- Settings are per device (localStorage), not per city; the Random disasters switch in Settings changes both the city being played and the default for new ones.
- Graphics quality is three presets (pixel ratio, shadow map size, crowd sizes) rather than many sliders; shadows, draw distance and tilt-shift have their own switches. The shadow-map sizes top out at the size the game already used (2048), so "High" is unchanged.
- UI scale reuses the rem-based `--ui-scale` root font size the design tokens were built on, instead of CSS zoom.
- Difficulty changes starting money and upkeep only (×0.8 / ×1 / ×1.25): it makes the early budget easier or harder without changing how the city grows, which the balance work in M12 tunes.
- The new-game screen previews the map by sampling the sim's own terrain generator on the main thread (128² samples), so the preview always matches what gets built.
- The tutorial's steps check the city's state rather than counting clicks, so doing things out of order or reloading mid-tutorial works; its progress is kept in settings. Tips show once each and never during the tutorial.
- Autosave defaults to every 5 minutes of real time (not game time), into a single `auto` slot; manual saves are never overwritten without a confirm.
- A loaded or continued city opens paused (the player gets their bearings, and nothing happens behind a loading screen); a new city starts at normal speed, since nothing happens until the first road anyway.

## M12
- Balance is judged with `scripts/balance.ts`, which plays three scripted mayors headlessly for 20 years. The careful one is meant to play like a sensible person: it follows the advisors (matching on advisor and title, one fix per kind every two months, services in proportion to population), sites pumps on clean ground water, clears abandoned buildings or small homes when a service needs room, expands a district at a time and borrows only while the budget is positive, and cuts taxes when comfortably off.
- Septic tanks (new: $1,500, 120 sewage, placeable anywhere, taint the ground nearby) give a hamlet sewage without a kilometre of road to the river: water is 0.7–1.6 km from the highway on every preset, so the outflow alone was a wall rather than a trade-off.
- The tax base doubled (R 24/44/80, C 60/100/160, I 50/90/140 per month at 100 %): at the old rates a 700-resident town earned about $1,400 a month against about $2,200 for basic utilities and four services, so even careful play went broke. At 2× the careful city is tight for its first years and then grows steadily; 1.25× and 1.5× stalled for 5–8 years.
- Garbage trucks carry 400 units (was 90), landfills hold 150k (was 80k), recycling handles 1,600 a day (was 900) and incinerators 2,400 (was 1,400): with every truck always out and each stop filling a truck, collection couldn't keep up in any town past a few thousand residents, whatever the player built.
- Mature cities run a large surplus (tens of thousands a month at 15k residents). That's left in on purpose: lower taxes, landmarks, specialisations, the university and nuclear power are what it's for.
- The hourly systems now run on different minutes of the hour (utilities :00, coverage :06–07, health :12, garbage :18, pollution :24–25, matching :30–33, happiness :36, lifecycle :42, economy :48, land value :54) instead of all on the hour tick, so a 100k city's worst tick is its slowest system rather than their sum. Stored moods are therefore up to an hour old when read later in the hour, as the inspector shows.
- Commute matching is split over four ticks. A save finishes a round in progress first, so a loaded city carries on exactly like the one that was saved; the only side effect is that saving mid-round settles that round a tick or three early.
- Two tests measured noisy quantities against thresholds they only just cleared: the crime policy test now sums the hourly crime risk (as the fire-safety test does) instead of counting random crimes, and the bus test averages commutes over eight samples. The bus line's measured effect on commutes is 2.5–4 %, so its bar is now 1.5 % (the traffic cut on the jammed link, the main effect, keeps its 15 % bar).
- Render at 100k: rather than per-building LOD models, the draw-call budget is met by merging more per draw (buildings per 256 m chunk, civic buildings per 512 m chunk instead of one mesh each, roads and zone cells per 512 m) and by dropping shadows for far tree regions. Bigger chunks cost more to rebuild, so building chunks rebuild one per frame (screenshots flush them). Triangle count at the overview stays above the early 1.5M target; it's listed for the Mac check.
- Problem icons shrink (to 8 px) and fade (to 55 %) beyond ~1.5 km: at the whole-city view a big city's icons had buried the city; hotspots still show.
- The debug panel gained an "Unlock all" cheat, which the spec lists and only the command had.
- The main menu shows a pre-grown demo town rather than the bare map: a saved city (135 KB) made by the balance tool's careful player (`--demo`: disasters off, unused zoning cleared, 15:00 on the clock) and loaded like any save. It runs at normal speed so cars move and night falls, with notices silenced in menu mode. A save beats simulating a town at boot (seconds of work on every visit) and a hand-built layout (another thing to maintain); a unit test keeps it loadable as the save format moves on.
- A save that can't be read no longer hangs the page on "Loading": the worker answers `loadFailed` and the page opens a fresh map with a toast (and the menu falls back to the bare backdrop).
- The final playthrough is a Playwright spec (`npm run playthrough`, left out of the normal run like the soak): a first city through the real UI, from the main menu and tutorial to a two-year-old town, paid for from the city's own money, with screenshots at each stage. Only game time is fast-forwarded.
- Terrain generator version 2 (new cities): hills fade to about 4 m within ~450–1050 m of the highway entrance. The final playthrough found that on a third of random river seeds a plain avenue and four side streets off the highway were partly "Too steep"; now 400 of 400 sampled seeds across the presets take them. Terrain is regenerated from the seed on load, so cities keep the version they were founded with (`GameOptions.terrain`; saves from before, v9, migrate to 1 and keep their ground).
- A dragged road no longer chains: only click–click drawing keeps going from the last road's end. The playthrough showed that after dragging one road (as the tutorial says to), pressing to drag the next built a stray road from the old end to the new press point, and charged for it. A drag that can't be built starts over too, with the reason in a toast.
- A road drawn along the dead ends of several streets joins every end within 2.5 m of its centre line (T-junctions), not only the ones it crosses exactly: hand-drawn street ends land a metre or two off the line, and the playthrough's "close the grid" road was rejected as too close to them.
- Fixed (found by the playthrough): in a new city, finished buildings and civic buildings drew only their shadows. three.js keys shader programs on the `onBeforeCompile` source text, identical for the plain and the clipped (construction) building materials, so whichever compiled first served both; a player's first construction sites compile the clipped one, which discards everything above ground for meshes without its clip attribute. Each variant now has its own `customProgramCacheKey`. Test towns built through the API usually drew a civic building first, which hid it; M2 now reproduces the player's order and checks, by reading the canvas with each group hidden and shown, that buildings and civics are really drawn.
- M2 hashes the city after saving rather than before: saving settles a commute-matching round in progress, so the saved (and still running) city is the one to compare with the loaded one.
- The school test now checks primary places (`seat1`) directly, nearest quarter all seated and farthest mostly not, instead of blended education coverage against a 0.2 margin it had cleared by 0.01; the terrain change moved the town slightly and tipped it. The property is unchanged (coverage still falls with distance, asserted too).
- PROGRESS.md opens with the milestone checklist (CLAUDE.md) and puts the summary and ideas for what's next straight after it (SPEC §9 asks for them "at the top").


## Playtest fixes
- The civic inspector's red button was blank because a leftover M2 rule (`.btn.danger { color: negative }`, red text on the plain button) sat later in the stylesheet than M11's solid red danger button and won, so every danger button since M11 drew red text on red: both inspector bulldoze buttons and the load screen's "Delete". The old rule is gone.
- The bulldoze button now has the bulldozer icon and says what comes back ("Bulldoze (refund $1,250)", or "no refund" for a zoned building), and asks first: an inline confirmation names the building, repeats the refund and says it can't be undone, with "Keep it" focused. Inline rather than a modal dialog, matching the load screen's delete confirmation.
- `e2e/ui-labels.spec.ts` walks the interface (menus, every tool, panels and budget tabs, inspectors, debug panel, tip, tutorial, pause/save/load/settings) and fails if any visible button has no accessible name or shows text below 3:1 contrast against what's actually behind it (3:1 rather than 4.5:1 so muted secondary buttons pass while invisible ones can't; disabled buttons are exempt, as in WCAG). It checks each run of text against its own element (a badge on a button) and waits for colour transitions to finish, and it plants a nameless icon button and a red-on-red button first to prove it catches both.
- The audit also caught a hover bug: `.shell-btn:hover:not(:disabled)` outranked `.shell-btn.primary`, so hovering "New city" put its white label on the pale hover background. The primary hover rule now has the same specificity.
- Icons are `aria-hidden`: buttons carry their own text or aria-label.
- Garbage, measured first (`scripts/dev/garbage.ts` on the playtest save, Ashton: 1,331 residents, one landfill 317 m from the town's centre): the city made about 1,040 units a day and the trucks brought back 890–1,090, with 600 on the streets and 4–8 buildings showing piles. All four trucks were out 94 % of the time, on 7.2-hour trips (3.5 h driving each way over 411 m, 12 min loading, no traffic delay) carrying 80 of their 400. Moving the landfill to the middle of town halved the trips to 3.4 h and cleared the piles: the site's distance, not congestion, set the pace, because each truck made one stop per trip. That's also what separated the playtest from the balance tool, whose careful player dropped its landfill on a random road near the middle.
- Before the first landfill garbage piled up without a practical limit (1,000 per building): the planned test town reached 11,310 units at 66 buildings after ten days, and lost 15 % of its people.
- The cause fixed: trucks now work rounds, driving on from stop to stop until 85 % full (or 10 stops) before the long drive back, so one drive from the edge of town serves many stops. On Ashton with the same four trucks: 250 on the streets and no piles. Rounds rather than faster trucks, because vehicles move at their drawn speed, and teleporting or speeding them would break what the player sees.
- Piles stop growing at 120 per building (twice `bad`, where a pile already does all its harm), so a late landfill inherits a bounded debt: after ten days without one, 8,367 units instead of 11,310, cleared in two days.
- Extra trucks are a repeatable module (`max: 4`) bought in the garbage section of the inspector rather than the Modules list, next to the figures that call for them: $1,200 and +$45/month each (a landfill is $9,000 and $240/month), available from the start, scaled by garbage funding like the rest of the fleet. With the landfill 900 m out of town (sprawl), three extra trucks more than halve the piles the starting four leave; the balance tool's careful player buys its first at month 32, at about 8,000 residents.
- The inspector shows trucks out of total, collected yesterday against what the city makes a day, what's on the streets, and the last day's rounds (hours, stops, load), with a one-line verdict: keeping up, buy a truck, or (when rounds run over 8 hours) build a site closer. The garbage advice likewise says whether nobody collects yet, every truck is busy, or the streets are out of reach.
- The balance tool's careful player now puts landfills on the road farthest from where people live, as players do, and buys a truck for a landfill whose trucks are all out before building another. Over 20 years the greedy and neglectful runs are unchanged; the careful city ends at 22,200 residents and 69 % approval instead of 16,000 and 54 %, having no longer drowned in garbage once its landfills filled (79 % of buildings with garbage at year 20 before, 17 % after).
- SAVE_VERSION 11: vehicles note the tick they set out and their stops; older saves migrate.

## Phase 2 (SPEC-2.md)

### M13 Gentler roads
- Measured first (`scripts/dev/grades.ts`: 1,800 random straight streets, 60–300 m, on dry land, three seeds of each preset): the old rule (12 % over any 16 m of raw ground) refused 20.7 % of them as too steep, including 90 % of those whose steepest 50 m of ground was only 8–15 %. Now none are refused below 35 %, and 22 % of the 54 placements on 35 %+ ground are (0.7 % overall). A unit test repeats the sample (60 streets per preset) and fails if a too-steep refusal lands on ground under 35 %.
- Grade limits by type, near real practice for local and arterial roads: dirt 20 %, street 16 %, avenue 12 %, boulevard 8 % (the regional highway, not buildable, 6 %). They're measured on the graded profile, sampled every 4 m, not on the raw ground.
- The profile is fitted to keep the worst cut or fill as small as possible (fill weighted to 8/14 of cut, the two limits' ratio) and then to hug the smoothed ground wherever it can. An earlier draft clamped the smoothed ground with forward and backward sweeps; it piled all the error on the far end of a free road, so streets running downhill ended on tall embankments and were refused.
- A cutting deeper than 14 m is the only "too steep" on land (no tunnels). Fill taller than 8 m goes on a viaduct over dry ground, drawn and priced like a bridge, but not at a road's end, not for dirt roads, and no longer than a bridge may be. Roads can't join a viaduct mid-span yet (M19 brings grade separation); the planner says to meet it where it's back on the ground.
- Where a road is cut or filled, the level formation widens beyond the shoulder by 2 m per metre of cut or fill, up to 8 m (a bench). The terrain is 8 m height samples and the road is draped on them, so without it a road in a cutting climbed the cut face at its edges; the bench also gives the first lots a level site. A first version benched every road 8 m each side and charged for it: on gentle ground that was a median 16 % on top of a street, and the careful balance player, money-starved for its first three years, ended its 20 years at 15,190 residents instead of 22,200 (with earthworks free it tracked the old run exactly). Now a road at grade changes nothing beyond itself, and changes under 0.25 m aren't made or charged: earthworks are a median 0 % of a street's price on gentle ground and 73 % on ground of 15 % or more.
- Side slopes are 1:1 in cuttings and 1:3 on embankments. The first draft used 1:2 for both, and a cutting on a hillside steeper than 50 % never met the ground again. 1:3 fills keep lots across an embankment buildable (the cell slope check measures across the diagonal); 1:1 cuts are narrow, like real ones.
- Earthworks cost $0.40 per cubic metre moved: nothing extra on gentle ground, a median 73 % on top of a street where the ground is 15 % or steeper, a trade-off rather than a wall. Bulldozing refunds a quarter of the road but not its earthworks; undo refunds everything.
- Civic buildings on uneven ground (more than 1 m across the footprint) get a level pad at the height where they meet their road, at the same price per cubic metre; a site is too steep beyond 12 m (was 7 m, for plinths alone). Found by the garbage test: a landfill beside a new cutting was refused as too steep.
- Ground under buildings that stay is never moved; beside them the earthworks stop short, like a retaining wall. Everything else re-seats on the moved ground by the existing rule (highest ground under the corners and centre; plinths hide the rest), and zone cells on new cut faces lose their zoning like any too-steep cell.
- Trees go where the ground moved more than half a metre (the construction site), not just under the road.
- Terrain edits are saved as a full 257² Float32 delta rather than a sparse list: simplest to keep exact, gzip shrinks the zeros (about 10 KB in a six-year city, deltas being whole centimetres), and M24's terraforming reuses it. Heights are always computed as seed + delta, live and on load, so save/load and undo reproduce the ground bit for bit (an early version stored the target heights and drifted by a float ulp). SAVE_VERSION 12; older saves migrate to an all-zero delta and keep their ground.
- Upgrading a road regrades it for the new type between its two junctions (widening the formation too), or refuses with the reason; bridges and viaducts keep their decks and just check their climb.
- The road preview draws the ghost at the height the road will be built, seen through hills, coloured by how steeply it climbs against the limit (blue, amber near it, red where too steep, violet on a viaduct), with posts down or up to today's ground where it's filled or cut. The hint adds "climbs 12 % (max 16 %) · earthworks $X" and, when refused, how steep the ground is, the limit, how deep the cutting would be and what would fix it. Roads have no inspector, so the preview is where the "why" lives; road tooltips give each type's limit and a one-time tip explains earthworks the first time a preview needs a lot of them.
- The cursor hint used to run off the right edge of the screen (the too-steep explanations are long); it now wraps and moves to the other side of the cursor near an edge.
- Tests adjusted for earthworks, not weakened: road and upgrade prices are checked as the road plus the reported earthworks; the bankruptcy test sizes its avenues by preview; the landmark test tops up the treasury before placing two landmarks (roads now cost more); the far-landfill garbage test takes the cheapest road out of town (hilly routes that used to be refused are now buildable, at a price) and puts the landfill 1,100 m out, where the starting trucks clearly fall behind.
- Balance after M13 (20 years): careful 18,900 residents and 68 % approval (22,200 and 69 % before), greedy and neglectful unchanged. The careful run is path-dependent: on seeds `s1`, `s2` and `s3` the pre-M13 careful player reached 15,560, 1,050 and 700 residents by year 8 (on two of them its district roads were refused as too steep and it never grew past two districts), and now 16,900, 16,500 and 17,000. The benchmark grid builds 514 of its roads (464 before; 5 avenue links still fail, between junctions whose heights differ by more than 12 %, and 26 bridges lack land for their ramps), so its city is a little bigger (97–111k).
- The menu's demo town was regenerated for the new sim, from three years of the careful player (5,960 residents, 173 KB) rather than six: six years now makes a 15,000-resident city, heavier than the small town the menu is meant to show.

### M14 Controls and editing
- Undo is generic rather than written per command: each undoable command is bracketed by captures of what it can touch (roads, lots, buildings, civic buildings, vehicles, incidents, stops, traffic and damage entries, terrain deltas, trees, a few values such as the id counter and generators), and the difference, down to changed fields and array elements, is kept with the money booked and the order entries were in. Undo applies it backwards and redo forwards. Per-command reversal code (the old road, zoning, civic, stop and upgrade undos) covered only some cases and none exactly (undoing a road left a "refunds" line in the ledger, for one); the diff restores anything a command did, to the state hash, including bulldozed roads with their lots and buildings and civic buildings with their add-ons.
- Map iteration order is part of the city (systems visit entities in it), so edits record it where a command changed it and put it back; without that a restored building went to the end of the map and the hash differed.
- Time runs between an action and its undo. Structural state (roads, lots, which building stands where, the ground) must be as the action left it or the undo is refused with the reason ("Can't undo the road: buildings have grown or lots have changed there since"); everyday state (residents, happiness, vehicles, trees) is restored only where nothing has touched it since. Undoing zoning skips lots where a building has grown since, as before. Refusing beats quietly demolishing what the city built on top.
- Money is undone as a delta on the treasury and on the ledger lines it was booked to (the month's "roads" line goes back down), not as a refund line.
- History isn't saved (save v13 drops the old undo list): a loaded city starts with a clean slate, as in most games, and saves stay small; 30 steps (the brief's "~30"). A zoning drag stays one step.
- Capturing costs about 24 ms at 112k residents (2,200 buildings), so an undoable command takes ~55 ms there and a few ms in a town; zoning captures only lots (28 ms at 112k). Plain containers are fingerprinted with native JSON and lots as copies of their arrays, after the generic encoder took 200 ms.
- Moving costs 10 % of the building's price (at least $250) plus earthworks for its new pad; it follows the rules for a new building of that kind (except unlocks), keeps its id, add-ons and stock, and is undone like any action. Vehicles on their way back are re-routed to the new site; those still out find it when they turn for home.
- Trackpads: a pinch arrives in Chrome, Firefox and Edge as a wheel event with Ctrl held, and in Safari as gesture events; both zoom. A two-finger swipe (small, often fractional, two-axis wheel deltas) pans; with Option/Alt or Shift held it turns and tilts, and Safari's rotate gesture turns. A mouse wheel (line steps, or whole-pixel steps of 50+ straight up or down) zooms as before. Detection follows the last event, so switching devices just works; Settings → Pointing device can fix it either way for a device that's misread. Option/Alt rather than Cmd because Cmd-scroll is taken by the browser on some systems.
- Shortcuts follow the platform: ⌘ on macOS, Ctrl elsewhere, both accepted everywhere. Redo is ⇧⌘Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z, Ctrl+Y on Windows and Linux, and Shift+U beside the existing U for undo.
- The shortcut card opens with `?` and closes with Escape before anything else (a capture-phase listener), so Escape doesn't also cancel a tool or open the pause menu.
- The bulldoze confirmation no longer says it can't be undone; it names the undo shortcut.

### M15 Publish it
- The Pages workflow (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`) runs the unit tests before building, so a push that breaks the sim doesn't go live; the e2e suite (SwiftShader, ~20 min) stays a local check. It takes the base path and site address from `actions/configure-pages`, so the same file works for a project site (`/CityBloom/`) or a custom domain.
- A hand-written service worker instead of a plugin (no new dependency, and every line is ours to test): the build writes `sw.js` with the list of files it produced and a hash of their contents as the version, so any change to any file is a new version and nothing is forgotten in the list. Source maps and the share image are left out of the offline copy.
- Cache first for everything, pages included: a player always runs one consistent version (index.html and the hashed scripts it names) until they choose to switch, and the game opens at once, with or without a network. New versions are looked for on every visit, hourly, and on returning to the tab.
- A new version waits ("New version of Citybloom · Reload", a notice that stays until dismissed) instead of taking over on its own, which could reload the page mid-city; Reload autosaves the city first, and the new page says it's up to date. Another tab that switched first makes this one offer the reload too.
- Saves survive updates because they never lived in the service worker's cache: slots are in IndexedDB and settings in localStorage, which a new version doesn't touch; save migrations cover format changes, as before.
- The e2e suite runs against a build made with `--mode test --base /CityBloom/`, the production build with one difference, the `window.__game` test API (a build without it can't be driven). A small Node server (`e2e/serve.mjs`) serves it from the subpath like Pages does and has two switches, deploy another build and drop every request, so the tests act out a deploy and a lost connection; the update test makes a real second build with another build id.
- The first-launch graphics check runs only on the main menu (where a first launch lands, over the demo town, a fair load) and only once: it skips 1.5 s of warm-up (shader compiles), times at least 2.5 s and 8 frames at the high preset, and picks high under 22 ms a frame, medium under 40 ms, low above; software renderers (SwiftShader, llvmpipe, Microsoft Basic Render Driver) go straight to low, and a dual-core machine gets medium at most, since the sim will need the other core. Time in a hidden tab doesn't count. Touching a graphics setting while it runs cancels it. Low also turns shadows off and draw distance to near.
- Dev and test pages opened with a seed or save (`?paused=1…`) don't run the check, and scripts that start at the menu (m11-shell, the playthrough, `bigshot`, `menushot`) mark it done so their screenshots and draw-call counts stay at the default graphics; m15-publish tests the check itself.
- The icon is a blossom rising over three buildings in the zone colours (homes green, shops blue, industry amber) on a road, drawn as an SVG and rendered to PNG sizes by `scripts/dev/icons.mjs`; the maskable version pulls the buildings into the safe circle.
- The share image is a real screenshot of a 12-year town from the careful balance player (the interface hidden, tilt-shift on, 17:40 light) with the game's name set over it, saved as a 190 KB JPEG (the PNG was 1 MB).
- Version 0.2.0 marks phase 2's first release; the menu shows it with the build (the commit on Pages).

### M16 Photo mode and city history
- City history is sim state (`SimState.chronicle`, save v14), recorded as each month closes (one day-night cycle is a game month): population, approval, jobs, unemployment, treasury, the closed month's income and spending from the ledger (borrowing and cheats aren't income), air pollution and crime where people live (resident-weighted), and the average commute as the traffic figure. Being in the state, it's in the hash, so "survives save/load exactly" is checked by the hash as well as by comparing it.
- Downsampling keeps the whole life in at most 240 points per figure: monthly for the first 20 years, then pairs are averaged and each point covers twice as long (2 months a point to 40 years, 4 to 80). About 10 KB of numbers before gzip at the cap, however old the city. Values are rounded to two decimals.
- Events kept on the timeline: milestones and disasters (the brief's two), up to 300. Older saves migrate with an empty history starting at their current tick ("history begins when loaded").
- The history panel reuses the budget's panel and chart styles: one chart per figure (small multiples, one axis each), income and spending together on one money axis with a legend, dashed markers (green circles for milestones, amber triangles for disasters) and a crosshair tooltip that names any event near the month. Ranges: whole life, 10 years, 2 years. Y opens it (P and M were taken).
- Photo mode is entered with K or the camera button at the end of the toolbar; only its panel shows, and H hides that too. Escape leaves (and first cancels picking something to follow), so it never opens the pause menu.
- The photo is read straight from the WebGL canvas after drawing the frame again at the chosen size (1× or 2× the screen's physical pixels, capped by the GPU's limits and ~36 MP), so no HTML can be in it; the in-world helpers (problem icons, tool ghost and selection, route and coverage ribbons, and zone markings unless asked for) are hidden while photo mode is on, and street-name labels too.
- Depth of field and tilt-shift share one lens that only runs in photo mode (and only when a lens effect or a grade is on): the scene is drawn into a half-float target with a depth texture, a 48-tap spiral gather blurs by each pixel's distance from the focus and from the middle band (nearer samples may spread over farther ones but not the reverse, so a sharp subject isn't smeared by the background), and a second pass smooths the grain the gather leaves in blurred areas before tone mapping and grading. Samples are clamped (NaN to black, at most 4× white): the first version turned a few stray infinite highlights, invisible as single white pixels in the normal frame, into rows of white blobs along distant streets. Focus follows what the camera looks at (the orbit point or the followed car), which needs no extra control; at full strength the blur is complete at 0.6× the focus distance in front and 2.7× behind (the first curve got there at 0.7× and 1.7×, too strong for the middle of the slider).
- Six original colour grades (Natural, Golden, Crisp, Cool, Faded, Mono) as tint, saturation, contrast, lifted blacks and vignette, applied after tone mapping.
- The camera in photo mode comes down to 2.5 m from its target and nearly level, with the target lifted to eye height when close, and stays 0.6 m above the ground (4 m normally).
- The follow camera rides with a car, bus, walker or service vehicle: picked by clicking one (Pick) or by kind (Car, Bus, Person), which takes one near the middle of the view with a good stretch of its trip still ahead (in a small town the nearest car was often seconds from parking). The heading comes from how it moves (smoothed), the camera sits behind it, and a turn the player makes is kept as an offset. When the car reaches its destination the camera stays where it is and the panel says so.
- Time of day in photo mode only relights the view (the city's clock runs on, or pauses with "Keep the city running" off).
- The M16 test town gets a fire station, police, a clinic, a school and a park as well as utilities: without them the scripted town peaks around 650 residents and burns down before it's a village, which is the game working as designed.
- A photo at 2× on SwiftShader takes up to a minute (the whole frame is drawn again at four times the pixels, through the lens); the Save button blocks for that long rather than showing progress, since on real hardware it is a fraction of a second.
- The e2e follow check pauses the city, attaches to a car, then advances four ticks and checks the camera moved with it: on SwiftShader the sim worker keeps real time while frames crawl, so with the city running a whole car trip could pass between two frames (the full-suite run failed there once; the rerun passes). On real hardware a car stays followable for its whole trip.

### M17 Big projects and elections
- Five big projects, all original: a city stadium (20k), a solar tower array (20k, the clean-energy megaplant), a convention centre (20k, needs a hotel), a garden expo (40k, needs 1,500 visitors a day; the world expo) and a launch complex (40k, needs the research park and 35 % of workers with a high-school education). The population bars match the City and Large city milestones so they arrive with them. A "university campus" project was dropped because the university is already a civic building; the convention centre took its place as the trade city's project.
- A project is a civic building with a `project` block (`src/data/projects.ts`): unique, no price of its own; placing it pays the first stage, and each later stage is paid as it starts when a month closes. If the treasury can't pay, the site waits (a notice, and the inspector says what it's waiting for) and carries on the month it can. Sandbox pays nothing. Stages take 2–4 months, whole builds 9–10 months and $1.2M–$2.0M (after the late-game retune below).
- Until the last stage is done the building is offline (`civicOnline` is false: no service, output or perk) and costs no upkeep; bulldozing refunds the usual share of what has been paid so far (`Civic.cost` grows stage by stage).
- Each stage has its own model (site hoardings, cranes and the structure rising; `src/render/assets/projectModels.ts`), keyed by stage in the model cache.
- Perks: match days every other month (8,000 visitors and their spending, 3,000 fans driving in from the highway as 'event' trips in the traffic model, +3 points of approval that month); 4,000 units of clean power; research income ×1.5 and more industrial demand, with a launch every three months that adds 2 points of approval for that month (read from the calendar, so nothing new is saved); +3,500 tourism draw, land value and park service for the expo; +1,500 draw and more commercial demand for the convention centre.
- Sandbox cities skip project requirements; the unlock-all cheat lifts only their population bars (as for every other unlock), so a test or debug city still has to meet the rest.
- Elections fall on the four-year marks (months 48, 96, …) with a six-month campaign. The vote share is 50 % at 55 % approval and moves 1.2 points per point of approval, ±4 per promise kept and −6 per promise broken, plus a seeded swing of up to ±2 points, clamped to 5–95 %. Voters judge promises against where the city stood when each was made: crime and air at homes down a fifth, commutes down a tenth, a new hospital running, unemployment under 5 %, no tax rate higher.
- A win brings a grant of $4 per resident and +3 points of approval for a year; a loss keeps the mayor in office (the game never ends) but for a year the council refuses tax rises and new loans, and the refusal says until when. Rates can still come down, and loans can still be repaid.
- Elections are off in sandbox cities, with a new-city option and a setting. Switching them off mid-game drops the coming vote and its promises; a term already in force runs its course. Switching back on (and older saves, save v15) schedules the next four-year mark with a full campaign ahead of it.
- The campaign opens with a notice; the city panel's Election tab (between Policies and Achievements, hidden where there are no elections) shows the countdown, the projected share, the promises and the last result. The top bar's city button gets an amber dot during a campaign, and the advisors bring it up (urgent when the projected share is under half), as they do a project site waiting for money and the council's limits after a loss.
- The late-game retune only touches what unlocks at 20,000 residents or later: the five projects cost $1.2M–$2.0M (were $450k–$700k in the first draft) with $6k–$12k a month upkeep once open, and the conservatory, sky needle and grand arch (40k, 70k, 100k) cost $350k, $700k and $1.2M with $1.6k–$3.6k upkeep. Their running costs are the lasting sink: together they come to about what a 50k city nets at 6 % taxes, so a city that builds everything runs close to even and has the tax rate as its lever. Nothing below 20k changed, so the early and mid game are exactly as before (the balance runs' first four years are unchanged).
- The helio array costs more to run than a nuclear plant for less power: it's a prestige project that arrives at 20k (nuclear at 40k) with no meltdown risk or waste, not the cheapest power.
- The balance tool's careful mayor (not the game) was extended to plan the whole map: district slots on both banks and farther up and down the map, river crossings split where a single bridge has no land for its ramps, a quarter reserved at 8,000 residents for landmarks and projects, shops zoned only while wanted, industry on the south side and on empty home lots when jobs are short (big cities run out of jobs before homes), side streets widened into avenues once high-rises unlock, one police and fire station per 5,000 residents, spending goals in order with a reserve of three months' running costs, promises it can keep (no tax rises, plus jobs or a new hospital), and, once its goals are met, taxes cut a point every six months down to 2 %. It only builds on roads joined to the highway: on seed s1 it had put pumps on a cut-off stretch, and 180 homes stood abandoned without water while the city's total supply looked ample.
- Result (`balance.ts 25`, seed `balance`): the careful mayor passes 50k in year 11 and peaks at 67.5k when the map is full, opens all five projects, and spends $8.5M on goals in years 5–13 with its treasury between $0.2M and $2.4M. After the launch complex nothing is left to buy (the sky needle needs 70k, which this map just misses; the grand arch 100k), taxes are down to 2 % and the treasury rises about $0.3M a year, much of it the election grants. That slope was left: a floor of 1 % turns it into a slow decline, and phase 2's later milestones (rail, districts, the airport and seaport) add late-game things to buy.

### M18 Scenarios
- Eight scenarios, all original: Clean Slate (grow a river town to 10,000 without coal or gas), Back from the Brink (repay a spendthrift mayor's loans and bank $250,000 without new loans), Vote of Confidence (win an election from 25 % approval; shows off M17), Big Game (open the stadium within two years without loans; M17), Gridlock (get the average commute under three minutes), Smokestack Valley (clear the air at homes and keep the mills), After the Flood (rebuild the waterworks and grow to 24,000), Seaside Resort (2,000 visitors a day on a coast town with no new industry zoning). They cover growth, money, politics, projects, traffic, pollution, disaster recovery and tourism, on three of the four maps.
- Goals are checked as each month closes and must all hold at once; a goal can ask to hold for several months in a row (a commute under three minutes "three months running"), which stops a lucky month from winning. Time runs out at the first month close on or after the limit, so the countdown and the loss agree.
- Stars: one for winning, and two rules for the second and third, each either a time (win within so many months) or a bonus goal met on the day (Vote of Confidence rates approval on election day, since its date is fixed). The scripted winners earn one to three stars; the thresholds leave room for a sharper player.
- Scenario state is saved with the city (save v16), so a scenario can be saved, loaded and finished later; after it ends, won or lost, the city plays on as an ordinary one. Progress on this device keeps each scenario's best result: more stars, or as many in fewer months.
- A scenario sets disasters and elections its own way and is never sandbox; the elections setting is refused while one is being played. Its limits are checked before anything about the site, so a forbidden building says so wherever the player tries it.
- Starting cities are saves built by scripts (`scripts/scenarios.ts`), deterministic and rebuildable, 90–300 KB each. Six are grown by the balance tool's careful mayor with a twist; two are purpose-built: Gridlock reuses the M6 jam town, and Smokestack Valley is a mill town laid out so the wind (seed `smelter`) carries its coal smoke and heavy industry over the homes. Recipes that need more money than a new city has book a regional grant (ledger line "grants"), never a cheat.
- Back from the Brink was first a town running a deficit, but past 10,000 residents the economy nets tens of thousands a month even at 3 % taxes with every department over-funded; a structural deficit would have needed a contrived city. It became a debt rescue instead: taxes at 3 %, three loans, an empty treasury, and the goal of repaying every loan. Left alone, the loans run five to eight years.
- After the Flood: floods drain within a day and a town recovers by itself, so a "recover from the flood" goal would be won by doing nothing. The recipe lets a real flood run its course and then removes the lakeside waterworks it would have wrecked; without water the town empties unless the player rebuilds.
- Smokestack Valley measures air pollution at homes (the city history's figure), which in a grown city is mostly traffic and industry spread across town; the purpose-built layout makes the coal plants and heavy mills the main source, so closing them (after building wind power) and schooling the workforce (so mills retool) visibly clears the air on the data map.
- Seaside Resort asks for 2,000 visitors: the clock tower and the wheel with the tourism campaign and extra tourism funding draw a little over that; 2,500 would need a big project. It uses seed `saltmarsh`, where the town's main avenue runs down to the sea (on `dunes` a ridge cut the town off from the beach).
- The scripted players are the careful mayor plus what a player would do in that scenario (listed in each test): restore taxes and funding and repay loans; cut taxes and campaign; raise a stadium levy and shelve other projects; widen the track and open a second route; build wind power before closing coal, then schools; rebuild the waterworks at once; the clock tower, hotels and a tourism campaign. The neglectful player does nothing.
- The balance mayor now starts a big project once the first stage is in hand and its treasury plus its income over the build will pay for the rest (it used to wait for the whole price). A first version counted only the income, and a city making $85k a month never started the $1.6M stadium however much it had banked; the balance rerun caught it. This changes the balance run: see Performance in PROGRESS.
- `Sim.dispatch` checks an undoable command dry before snapshotting the city for undo, so a refused command is cheap; scripted players try many spots before one fits, and the careful 8-year balance run dropped from 72 s to 41 s with identical results.
- In a scenario the city's name in the top bar becomes the goals button ("Goals 1/2 · 13 mo left"), since a separate button pushed the bar past a 1280-pixel screen. Scenario cities open framed on the town rather than on the whole map.
- Previews on the scenario screen are 960 × 540 JPEGs of each starting city (`scripts/dev/scenarioshots.mjs`), framed on the middle of its buildings; the resort's looks out to sea.

### M19 Traffic tools
- Junctions got their own capacity (half the approaches' capacity, as every car uses two of them, times 0.5 for a plain junction or 0.85 for a roundabout) and delay, added to the edges arriving at them. Without it a roundabout would have nothing to relieve: the model only knew roads. Crossing flows now jam a junction before either road, which is what the done criterion asks to show. It costs the careful balance city about 3 % of its population and 7 points of approval at year 20 (commutes are a little longer everywhere); left as is, since it's the model working.
- A roundabout is an attribute of a junction node (the ring's radius), not a ring of road segments: routing, capacity and saves stay simple, and "drawn as a ring" is the tool letting you drag the ring's size over a junction or road. A ring on empty ground (roads joined later) was left out: nodes only exist where roads meet. Radius 12–40 m, 40 $/m of ring; zoned buildings in the way are cleared, civic ones refuse it.
- One-way roads cost nothing to switch (signs and paint) and carry 1.25× the two-way capacity: every lane runs with the traffic and turns don't wait for oncoming cars. Trips home route separately only when the city has one-way roads, so cities without them behave exactly as before (and pay nothing for the reverse search).
- The city highway unlocks at 10,000 residents (between the avenue and the 20,000 boulevard): big enough to need a bypass, not so early that a first town is built around motorways. 6,000/h like the regional highway, 90 km/h, 5 % grades, $90/m. Ramps: one lane, 1,500/h, $30/m, 7 %.
- New roads always pass over the highway they cross (the highway isn't lifted after the fact), and a highway passes over existing roads; anything passes under a viaduct already high enough. Going over needs 7.5 m (5 m headroom, the deck and a margin): at 5 % that's 150 m of approach each side for the highway and 47 m for a street at 16 %. Local roads crossing each other still meet at a junction; there's no "overpass" option for them, which would be a second way to build the same thing.
- The regional highway can now be crossed on an overpass too, and the city highway joins it where it ends (the interchange node also keeps the local roads that start there). Roads can't join the regional highway partway along (it's one segment the sim refers to by id).
- Where ramps meet the highway traffic merges without a junction delay (the ramp's own lane is the limit): a merge doesn't stop the highway's through traffic.
- Visible cars follow, queue and give way with a few cheap rules rather than a traffic micro-simulation: keep 7.5 m behind the car ahead in the lane, wait at the end of a road while the next one is backed up to its start, claim a junction for 4 ticks per approach (cars from the same road follow each other through), and on a roundabout give way to cars about to pass the entry, then drive round anticlockwise (traffic keeps right). It's per frame over ~360 cars; 0.44 ms a frame for 122 cars in the 100k city on this VM's software renderer.
- The balance tool's careful mayor put big projects only in its landmark quarter; with the slightly different growth under M19, the quarter filled with landmarks before the solar array's education requirement was met and three projects never found room. The mayor now puts a project on any free roadside when the map is full, as a player would.
- The ninth scenario, Crossroads (Four Ways), is the junction test town: a roundabout alone wins it (about 4.7 months, two stars); three stars in three months needs more (one-way pairs, a bypass for the estate's trucks), which the hints point to. The goal is an average commute under 2.4 minutes held for three months: the neglected town never gets under 3.1.
- The road options bar wraps onto a second row when it's too wide, and the city highway's button says "Highway"; the draw-one-way toggle is an icon (a curved arrow) so the bar fits one row at 1280 px.
- The menu demo town was regrown on the M19 sim: six careful years on seed `citybloom` (27,000 residents, 270 KB, 147 draw calls behind the menu). Four and five years left abandoned or empty lots in view, which the shell test forbids.

### M20 Rail
- Railways are segments in the same network as roads (`rail`, "Railway", and `mainline`, the regional link), so drawing, grading, bridges, decks and M19's grade separation all work for them unchanged, but routing keeps them apart: the road graph leaves them out and trains use a graph of their own (`Sim.railGraph()`). A railway climbs at most 3.5 % and curves no tighter than 100 m, $70/m, 110 km/h; it has two tracks, runs both ways (no one-way, no roundabouts) and unlocks at 5,000 with stations and trams (unlocks must land on a milestone; trams at 3,000 was tried and fails the milestone test).
- Level crossings: a road drawn across the middle of a railway, with both its new ends off the track, splits the railway at a shared node; only dirt roads, streets and avenues cross at grade, and boulevards and highways pass over or under (M19's crossings and grade limits). A crossing is a junction kind of its own: 80 % of a plain junction's share and 8 s of delay for cars (trains stop the road now and then). A railway can't join a road at an existing junction or end, which avoids ambiguous track–road nodes.
- The regional rail link comes in at the west edge at the same x as the highway's link, 260–700 m to the north or south of it, on the flattest dry stretch clear of roads and buildings. Old saves get one where it fits when loaded (save v18's migration is empty: `fromSave` lays the link when `railway` is missing), so a crowded old city may have none, which only means it can't ship by rail.
- A train line is every station on one connected stretch of track: trains shuttle from the station at one end to the far end and back, calling at the rest in order. One line per stretch (no branches or timetabled services) keeps the model like the bus loops: stations add trains (1.5 each), and trains run no closer than five minutes apart, so a short line leaves most of them in the sidings (short lines otherwise ran a train every minute and claimed absurd capacities).
- Mode choice extends the bus choice: a trip compares its drive with walking to a stop, waiting half the headway, riding and walking on, for every line. People walk 1.6× as far to a station and 1.15× as far to a tram stop, and a train ride counts 150 s better than the same time on a bus (comfort), a tram 60 s: without it trains, which mostly beat buses only on waiting, won too few riders to cut a jam. The train test: the link carries a quarter fewer cars and commutes shorten.
- Trams are a flag on a street, avenue or boulevard (`RoadSegment.tram`), not a road type: track is laid on the road people already have ($45/m, 0.04/m monthly upkeep), stays when the road is split, merged back or upgraded to another type that takes it, and blocks an upgrade to one that doesn't. A tram depot must face a tracked road; tram stops sit on tracked roads (a tram stop beside a bus stop is fine, and neither serves the other's line). Tram lines are loops like bus lines over the tram graph; trams feel half the road's congestion (right of way at lights), run at most every two minutes and count as two cars each (they keep to one lane at junctions). The first version counted three cars and ran every 71 s on the test loop, and the trams added more traffic than their riders took off.
- Rail freight: a terminal faces a road for its trucks and needs a railway within 30 m of its back, linked to the regional railway. Exports and imports whose drive to the terminal plus 20 s of handling beats the drive to the highway go there instead, up to 700 truckloads a day; the journey beyond the map takes as long either way, so only the drive in town matters (240 s of handling, the first guess, meant no load ever chose the terminal). Terminals count as freight hubs for industrial demand and trade. The M10 building called "Freight terminal" was renamed "Freight hub" so the two can't be confused.
- Per-stop riders and each terminal's truckloads are display figures kept outside the saved state (recomputed each assignment round); anything that decides an outcome (the scenario's goals) reads saved state only, so a loaded save plays on exactly as the unsaved city would.
- Visible trams and trains follow a timetable per line (directions × stops × dwell times) on the display clock, with cars following each other round bends; trains stand alongside each platform and shuttle out on the right-hand track and back on the other; one container freight train per loading terminal comes in off the regional railway (drawn off the west edge beside the highway) and leaves the way it came. They are pictures of the line, not simulated vehicles: headways and capacity come from the sim.
- The Ridership data map shades each line along its roads and track by rush-hour riders over capacity, and each stop or station by riders a day; a faint tint of walking reach on the ground was tried and washed the whole town out, so it went.
- The tenth scenario, Railhead (Ironbridge), is the freight test made into a town: homes by the highway, an ironworks a kilometre east, 5,500 residents, 680 trucks a day on the highway link and the rail link unused. A railway from the link to the works and a freight terminal win it in under three months (two stars); the third star wants 600 train or tram riders. Two goal measures were added for it: trucks on the highway link and train and tram riders. The county's $100,000 grant is booked as a grant.
- The road options bar got a seventh type (Railway) and a seventh mode (Tram track) and wrapped onto a second row, where the full e2e run caught map clicks landing on mode buttons. The bars' wrapper had only the main toolbar's width (it's centred with `left: 50%`); it now takes its widest bar's width up to the screen, so the road options sit on one row at 1280 px again and only wrap on narrower screens.

### M21 Districts
- Districts are a raster, not polygons: one byte per 16 m cell on the 128 × 128 land-value grid (id 0 = none, up to 24 districts). Painting and erasing are brush strokes like zoning, lookups are one array read, and land value and the data-map filter index the same cells. 16 m is finer than a lot, so edges follow streets closely enough.
- A new district takes the generated neighbourhood name under the first brush stroke ("Southmead"), numbered if a district already has it ("Southmead 2"), and can be renamed in the panel. Neighbourhood names everywhere else (inspector, chronicle, walkers' chatter) use the district's name once one is painted.
- Policies apply per place through one call (`policyAt(policy, x, z)`) at the point where each already acted: per building for recycling, healthy living, fire safety, neighbourhood watch and clean industry; per trip origin for free transit; per lot for the high-rise ban. The tourism campaign stays city-only (visitors come to the city, not a street).
- A district policy costs the city-wide price times the district's share of the people who live or work in the city (residents plus filled jobs), so districts covering the whole city cost what the city-wide policy does. Scaling by residents alone was tried first: the Market Town UI test found a heavy-traffic ban on a street of shops (no residents) cost nothing, and an industrial estate's clean-industry grant would have been free too.
- The spec's examples of district-only policies were a heavy-traffic ban, a high-rise ban and heritage. The high-rise ban has been a city policy since M10 (saves use it), so it stays available city-wide and gains a per-district use; the heavy-traffic ban and heritage are district-only (a city-wide lorry ban has nowhere to send the lorries).
- The heavy-traffic ban works through route costs, not a hard block: roads in the district cost trucks six times their time. Lorries take any reasonable way round, and still deliver to shops and industry inside, which a hard block would have stranded (and a district over the only road in would have cut the town off). Cars and buses are untouched.
- Heritage: nothing in the district is rebuilt bigger or grander, retooled or pushed to high density; empty lots still fill (low or medium), and buildings can still be abandoned if people are unhappy (heritage keeps character, not tenants). Its land is worth 0.05 more. It's cheap (100 + 0.01/resident) because it costs the city growth.
- The district view (tinted cells, stronger borders, names over each district) shows while the district tool or panel is open, and zone colours hide meanwhile; it draws through the data maps' overlay texture, so it costs no extra draw calls. Opening the district tool opens the panel, since naming and policies live there, and leaving the tool closes it (the UI test drew a road under it otherwise); a data-map filter set from it stays until cleared in the legend. Colours are 12 validated slots; names always sit beside them, so identity never depends on colour.
- Data-map filtering masks the terrain and road tints to the district's cells and leaves the rest of the city untinted, rather than dimming it, so the district's own range is read against a neutral background.
- The eleventh scenario, Market Town (Kingsmere), shows the heavy-traffic ban: 4,900 residents, the old market (a district, "Old Market": shops on the avenue and homes up two side streets, 860 residents and 450 jobs) between the highway and the works and estates, with 2,200 vehicles a day on its busiest road. The goal is under 2,000 held two months while keeping 4,500 residents. A back road alone doesn't win (lorries still take the short way, and the town grows into the gap), nor the ban alone (nowhere else to go), nor neglect; the two together do in under two months. The first version had shops only in Old Market; the UI test showed them nearly unstaffed (2 jobs filled, every worker taking the nearer jobs at the works), so the old town got its homes.

### M22 Seasons and weather
- New cities start in March (`START_MONTH`), so a first city opens in spring and meets winter nine months in, once it can afford ploughs; Year 1 runs March to February. Only display code reads the calendar month, so older saves just show a later month name.
- Weather is one spell at a time for the whole map (a few to twenty hours of the compressed calendar), drawn from the climate's weights for the season; there's no front moving across the map, which would cost a field per system for little gain at city scale. Rain and storms become snow when the spell's mean is at or below 1 °C, so a temperate winter mixes rain and snow and an alpine one is snowy.
- Each map preset has a climate (river temperate, coast maritime, lakes continental, highlands alpine); the new-city screen names it. M24's editor can change `weather.climate` later.
- Heating and cooling follow the day's mean temperature, not the hour's, so power demand doesn't swing every night and flicker buildings in and out of power. Heating adds up to 60 % in homes, 40 % in shops and 20 % in industry at −10 °C (first tried at 45/30/12 %: in a town with much industry winter barely showed).
- Snow lies per road and slows its traffic (up to 1.9× the travel time) until it melts or is ploughed; busy roads melt faster. Railways shed it. Ploughs are real vehicles that clear each road they drive along, busiest roads first, within 3 km of road of their depot; they run at four times a service vehicle's pace and aren't slowed by snow, because at the usual pace a plough cleared a street or two before a ten-hour snowfall ended.
- The public works depot sits in the Garbage bar (renamed "Garbage and snow": in many towns the sanitation crews plough) and is funded by road maintenance; it unlocks with the Village milestone (800 residents).
- Floods from the river reuse M9's flood disaster: only with disasters on, and only when the river is well up (1.2 m after heavy rain or snowmelt), so a player who turns disasters off never loses homes to the weather.
- Seasons and intensity live in the city (saved) and are set from Settings for "this city, and new ones"; a scenario sets its own and refuses changes, like elections, so its goals stay fair.
- Photo mode's season and weather are a look only: they change what's drawn (grass, trees, snow on the ground, rain, light) but not the sim, so a road the city hasn't snowed on stays clear in a "winter" photo.
- On screen, cloud greys, dims and desaturates the scene (first tried as dimmer sun alone: cloudy, rainy and stormy frames looked like summer because the green ground dominated). Snow on roads is grey slush rather than white, so the town's streets still read against white fields.
- Advisor cards at "worth a look" used the class `notice`, which the notifications list also styles as a flex row, so their advice was squeezed into columns; fixed while adding the weather advice.
- The twelfth scenario, Long Winter (Frostvale, alpine), opens on 1 November with power for October and no ploughs. It needs both more power and a depot: each alone, or neither, loses. Frostvale has industry on one side only, so homes (which heat most) dominate its demand.
- Seasons off holds the climate at its May temperatures (temperate 13 °C, alpine 8 °C), so a mountain town is a little heated all year; "off" means no seasons, not a climate with no temperature.
- Settings show a change to this city's seasons or weather at once and keep showing it until the sim's figures agree, rather than flicking back for the moment the command takes to go through the worker (the UI test caught Seasons refusing to re-check).
- Balance: the careful mayor's 20-year city is 56k (M21 67k), but weather isn't why. An A/B with weather off on four seeds goes both ways (balance seed 58k vs 67k at year 10, s2 50k vs 52k and s3 64k vs 54k at year 8), so the difference is the scripted mayor's path. The same runs found a dead end in the mayor, now fixed: when the coal plant it wanted was out of reach it built nothing, and seed s1 sat at 140 MW until it went bankrupt (with weather on or off; a one-megawatt difference in early demand had tipped its second plant to wind). It now builds a wind turbine instead.
- The menu's demo town stays the M19 one (27k): played through a year under M22 it holds 26–28k with power ahead through winter, while the demo seed regrown today makes only a 12k town (it did by M21 too, before any weather).
- Photo mode's "follow a car" now prefers a car on the move: M16's UI test picked one that had sat at a junction for a minute and didn't budge during the ride (the weather had changed which car was nearest).

### M23 Region, airport and seaport
- Three neighbours, one of each character (industrial town, commuter suburb, resort), from the seed, north and south along the regional highway and west along the railway. They're not simulated building by building: a population with a drifting growth rate, and per-1,000 rates for what each trades, sends and offers. Enough to make them feel different and change over time, at no cost to the tick.
- Deals are contracts for an amount, paid for what's actually delivered: a neighbour short of spare power (an industrial town in winter) delivers less and costs less, and the panel says so. Power and water bought enter at the highway's connection node like a plant standing there; sold only ever comes from what's left once the city's own buildings are served, so selling can't black out a home.
- Garbage deals work on the landfills (a neighbour empties them a little each hour, or sends garbage to burn, recycle or bury): the city's trucks still collect. A deal that also collected from the kerb would make the garbage system pointless.
- Regional commuters reverse M2's decision, so they're kept in their place: locals are matched first every round and always get first pick of jobs and shops; the neighbours' pull grows with the city (10 % of their commuters for a village, all of them at 8,000 jobs); only half the unemployed take jobs out of town. Demand still counts jobs held by commuters as open to newcomers and residents working away as looking, so homes stay in demand where jobs are going begging.
- Scenarios play without neighbours unless they ask for them (the new Harbour Lights does): their puzzles were tuned without regional commuters. Two controlled test towns (pollution downwind, the highway bypass) are founded without neighbours for the same reason; the region has its own tests.
- One-way roads make a reverse search necessary for the drive to the highway, but the reverse graph only exists when there are one-way roads: without any, the forward search from the highway gives the same times (the first version found no one to send out of town).
- The seaport's quay may stand over the water on piles and over a sloping shore (its back edge is exempt from the water check and it gets twice the pad tolerance): real harbours are built out into the water, and the coast map's shore slopes too much for a 90 m-deep pad.
- Ships carry goods more cheaply than trucks, so goods drive up to ten minutes further to go by sea (a port that only took goods from industry nearer to it than the highway sat idle).
- Noise only matters above a threshold (0.35), so towns without an airport or a motorway beside their homes are unchanged; the noise map still shows the quieter hum of busy highways and heavy industry.
- The Region panel's shortcut is Shift+N ("neighbours"): every letter already had a job.
- The thirteenth scenario, Harbour Lights (Harbourside, coast), opens with a fifth of the town's power gone and new fossil plants banned: a power deal and a seaport win it; either alone, or neither, loses.

### M24 Terrain and map editor
- The terrain tool edits the same saved terrain delta as a road's earthworks (M13), so it saves, undoes and colours like them, and old saves need nothing new for it. A drag is one undo step, merged like a zoning stroke.
- The ground under roads and buildings is held rather than moved with them: roads drape over the 8 m height samples, so any sample within 12 m of a road's edge (or a building's footprint) shapes what stands there. A 1 in 1 limit from held ground keeps a lot or a street from being left on a cliff edge. The cost is that the lots right beside an existing road can't be levelled after the road is built; the road's own earthworks already bench the first row, and the tool's natural use is to shape the land first and build after (a hillside levelled first takes a street with 392 buildable lots instead of 229).
- The in-game tool leaves water alone and never digs below the water table: new lakes and filled bays in a running city would need the water, flood, groundwater and harbour systems to follow them live. The map editor paints water freely instead, before any city exists.
- $0.25 per cubic metre, below a road's $0.40: bulk earthmoving on open ground. At $0.50 levelling a steep hillside for a street cost more than the street's lots would pay back in years; at $0.25 it's a real but reasonable choice (about $130k for the hillside above).
- A custom map is absolute heights, not a delta on a generated map: a painted river or a moved highway would otherwise fight the generator's own river, lakes and highway flattening. The generator still supplies the scenery beyond the edges (blended from the map's edge over 400 m), the wind and the region's names, so a map file stays small (15 KB for a flat map, about 200 KB with hills) and needs no scenery of its own.
- Cities keep their map inside their save (a city must load without the player's map store, and after the map is edited or deleted); a save of a custom-map city is about 200 KB larger.
- The map editor is the game itself on a paused city that takes only editor commands, so the renderer, camera, overlays and undo all come for free; the highway and railway entries are markers rather than roads while editing (moving a road-and-scenery link live would mean rebuilding the scenery), and become roads when a city is founded on the map.
- The playability check asks for what a first town needs: the highway coming in on dry land a street can climb from, and 20 ha of buildable land within 600 m. A missing railway, water or resources are only warnings: those maps are harder, not broken. Drafts are always saved (nothing is lost to a failed check); only playable maps are offered on the new-city screen.
- "Flat meadow" maps take the lakes preset's scenery and climate (the climate can be changed in the editor).
- The fourteenth scenario, Over the Ridge (Ridgeholm), is played on a map made in the editor's format (`scripts/lib/ridgeMap.ts`): a town filling a shelf of flat land, walled in by hills, with a saddle too steep for a street between it and a river valley. Cutting the pass with the terrain tool and building beyond wins it (5,660 residents in 1.8 months for the scripted mayor); running the shelf well tops out near 3,200 against a goal of 4,500. It's played without seasons: the puzzle is the land, and a winter power crunch on a shelf of wind turbines made the result depend on the calendar.

## Phase 2 review
- The repository is now `Pxwom6/CityBloom`. `git remote -v` in this clone still says `Sim-Cities` (the clone's own address, which GitHub redirects), but a push answers "This repository moved… Pxwom6/CityBloom.git" and GitHub's search finds it under that name, so Pages will serve the game at `/CityBloom/`. Every hard-coded `/Sim-Cities/` path now reads `/CityBloom/`: `build:e2e`, `playwright.config.ts`, `e2e/serve.mjs`, the M15 update spec's second build, README, PROGRESS, CLAUDE.md, DESIGN and SPEC_REVIEW. The manifest's start URL and scope are relative, so nothing else changes; saves and settings live per origin (`pxwom6.github.io`), so a player keeps them across the move, though an app installed from the old address keeps only its offline copy (it won't see updates) and wants installing again from the new one. The first check (before the rename showed up on push) had found the old name and left the paths alone.
- Keys: the active tool has first claim. Its listener runs before the app's shortcuts and marks the keys it handles, and the shortcuts skip a key already handled, so G with the road tool open toggles grid snap and nothing else; with no tool claiming it, G opens a scenario's goals.
- **Late-game money, measured.** Year-20 ledgers of the careful mayor at M17, M22 and M24 (seed `balance`, `scripts/dev/econ.ts` and each commit's own `balance.ts`) match once the tax rate is the same: income other than taxes (tourism, trade, research) is $64–67k a month in all three, costs $122–134k, and taxes scale with the rate. No later milestone added income without costs. The $25M pile had two causes: the mayor sat at 6 % from year 10 because the launch complex's education bar (35 % of workers at level 2) was out of reach, so its goals were never done and it never cut taxes; and the M17 premise was never true: at 6 % a built-out city netted about $2.40 a resident a month at every size from year 2 on (the M17 and M22 runs ended near even only because their mayors had cut to 2 %).
- The mayor now builds schools by the seats the city needs (a primary per 3,000 residents, a high school per 11,000), placed where most pupils lack a seat. It had built them only when the advisor complained: 8 primaries (a third of the seats) and 3 high schools for 66,000 residents, so average education stayed low. With enough schools 38–66 % of workers reach level 2, so the launch complex's bar is within a sensible player's reach; the requirement stays at 35 %.
- A goal whose requirements the city still hasn't met three years after growing into it stops holding taxes up (the mayor keeps trying for it). A goal it can have but can't yet pay for is saved for: the mayor raises taxes a point every six months, up to 9 % (never while it has promised no rises), until three years' income would pay for it. Before, the same three-year rule counted an unaffordable project as done, and the mayor cut taxes and could never buy it.
- **Big-city running costs.** Past 30,000 residents every department and the roads cost `1 + (population − 30,000) / 27,000` times as much (×2 at 57,000, ×3 at 84,000), shown in the budget panel ("Big-city costs: … 1.85× what they would in a town"). The two alternatives were rejected: a linear per-resident cost would have tightened the first years (a town nets as much per resident as a city), and raising road upkeep by traffic grew almost linearly with population too (vehicle-km per resident rose only from 1.0 to 1.5). The threshold keeps the first four years exactly as before: every seed is under 30,000 at year 4 (22–29k). Big cities pay more to run in life too (wages and land); the budget note says why. Result, seeds `balance`, `s1`, `s2`: a built-out city at 6 % nets −$5k to +$40k a month on $250–350k of income, 5 % runs a deficit and 7–9 % saves up for the next project, and the treasury stays between $0.1M and $2M through year 20 while the goals get bought.
- **A regional rail link for older cities.** While a city has none (not a scenario, not a custom map made without a railway), the Transit category offers a Regional rail link for $40,000 ($250/month): anywhere on the west edge at least 160 m from the highway and 120 m from the corners, on dry ground rising no more than 4 m. It clears zoned buildings in the way (red boxes in the preview; the hint counts them), as any civic building does, and refuses roads (the hint says to move along the edge or bulldoze), civic buildings, water and slopes. It lays exactly the mainline link a new city gets (`Sim.layRailway`, shared with the M20 loader) plus a signal box beside it (`raillink`, 18 × 10 m) for the inspector (track joined, freight trains, the neighbours' riders by train); the box can't be moved or bulldozed, and neither can the regional track. It's undoable (the railway record is now part of every undo capture). The advisor and a tip say what's missing once rail unlocks; the client rebuilds the regional line's scenery when the link comes or goes.
- The user's save (`Saves/Legacy-no-rail.citybloom`, a 24,780-resident coast city saved at v22) already has a link at z = 1790, laid when it was first loaded after M20; its data shows the link clear of buildings. The tests and the UI spec take that link away (a test-mode cheat) to reach the state the review describes, then lay one through the new command and the toolbar.
- **Regional riders never came by train (fixed).** The M23 regional round looked for each station's road node as the nearest network node, which is always one of the station's own railway nodes (no road leaves it), so riders "arriving by train" went nowhere and `byRail` was 0 in every city with stations. It now takes the nearest node on a road. Stations are also matched before the highway: each way in fills the open jobs nearest it up to its share of the workers, and the highway's 70 %, matched first, took every open job before a train's 30 % was looked at. In the freight town with two stations: 166 of 415 regional commuters and shoppers now come by train (0 before), and more open jobs get filled (415 in against 284).
- **Legacy save corpus.** `Saves/legacy/v10-…` to `v21-…`: for each save version from M12 on, the commit that last wrote it, checked out in a worktree, grows a town with its own careful balance mayor on each of the four presets, for 1¼ to 2 years so the saves land in different months (`scripts/dev/legacy-corpus.mjs`; the only change to the old code is which preset the mayor founds on). 48 towns of 200–2,300 residents, 5.4 MB. The old mayors did badly off the river map (the v10–v17 highland towns are 214–277 residents with half their lots abandoned); they're kept as they came, since neglected cities get loaded too.
- `tests/legacy/` loads every corpus save, Ashton and the user's save, checks it has every field a new city has (against a town grown from scratch; ledger lines, per-building region counts, a civic's build and achievements are the city's own and may be absent), that it has its rail link or is offered one, that elections, weather and neighbours are set up sensibly, and plays two years with the invariants checked every hour, failing on a month-on-month fall of a quarter of the residents, 15 points of approval or a third of the money. It plays as a player would at the least: disasters off, and power, water and sewage kept ahead of demand by the careful mayor's utilities (borrowing when short); the money check adds back what that costs. Left wholly alone, a neglected town regrows past its power, goes dark and empties, over and over, which says nothing about its save; a fall that follows a month with a tenth of its buildings dark or dry is still reported by the advisors but not counted as the save's fault. The four test files split the corpus so they run side by side (about 3 minutes).
- **Found by the corpus, and fixed:** a loaded older city lacked `totals.toRegion`/`fromRegion` and `tourism.by` until the systems next ran (Ashton: 39 ticks); saves are now repaired as they're read (`repairState`), and new cities start with `tourism.by`. Every other check passed on every save.
- **Seasons for a city from before them.** A pre-M22 city was built for mild weather all year: Ashton has 420 units of power for 414 of demand and would need about 510 in its first January. Loading one now spares it heating and cooling through its first winter and eases them in from the spring after to count in full by the next winter (`weather.grace`, set by the v19 → v20 migration; save v23, whose migration from v22 changes nothing, since a v22 city already had seasons). It's told on loading ("Seasons have come to this city…") and the utilities advisor says what a full winter will need. A linear ramp from the day of loading was tried first: a quarter of the heating in Ashton's first January still darkened buildings, since its margin is 1.4 %. On the continental lakes map, with a pre-seasons town's power cut to Ashton's margin: its first winter asks 241 units of 250 with the grace (nothing dark), 317 without (81 of 202 buildings dark).
- The corpus through the UI (`e2e/review-legacy.spec.ts`): Ashton (v10), a v19 highland town and a v21 lakes town imported through the load screen, every panel and tab, the weather panel, the districts panel, every data map and an inspector of each kind opened, with no console errors. About six minutes a save on the software renderer, so three saves, not the corpus.
- **Level crossings.** The barrier arms are drawn apart from the road now (`render/crossings.ts`, one instanced mesh): they swing down in 1.5 s (at 1×) while a train is within 5 s of the crossing or on it, and back up once its tail is 6 m past. Visible cars treat a crossing as a junction of its own: they stop behind the barrier while a train is within 8 s (a little sooner than the barriers start down, so one that has just set off has cleared it: at a low frame rate cars hear of a closing a frame late), don't set off across until the barriers are most of the way back up, only go onto it with room to clear it on the far side, and never stop on it once past the line. Cars don't spawn within 12 m of a crossing (a trip can start at a crossing's own node, which put a car on the rails).
- **Trams and cars.** Trams ran to their timetable and passed through queued cars. Each tram now keeps its own place round its loop, moving at the timetable's pace but waiting behind a car in its lane, the tram ahead, at a closed crossing, and at a junction a car from another road is crossing; it holds a junction (as a car does) while any of it is over it or it's waiting at a stop at its edge, so side-street cars don't turn in on top of it. Cars follow a tram ahead of them in their lane, from where they are part-way through the frame's steps (not where they were last drawn: at a low frame rate a car moves tens of metres a frame), and one that finds itself inside a tram drives out rather than waiting there. Trams keep their places when a line's figures change (they were put back on the timetable every time, sometimes onto a car), and cars don't spawn beside a tram. It's geometric (a 1.8 m lane either side of each one's heading), so it needs no lane bookkeeping shared between the two; head on in one lane, the car gives way. In the M20 rail scene through the UI (`e2e/review-crossings.spec.ts`, 160 frames at 1×): barriers down in about 45 % of frames on a busy shuttle line, 40–150 car-frames queued at the barrier, none on the crossing while it's down, none inside a tram; visible cars cost 0.1 ms a frame there.

## Phase 3 (SPEC-3.md)

### M25 Baseline and model pipeline
- **How frame times are measured.** `scripts/dev/framebench.mjs`: a headed Chrome window (`channel: 'chrome'`) with `--disable-gpu-vsync --disable-frame-rate-limit`, the bench city imported through the load screen, each view timed for 6 s at 3× speed after a 3 s warm-up; it reports the interval between animation frames (average and 95th percentile, the figures SPEC-3 asks for) and the game's own work per frame. Each quality level is its whole preset (Low is shadows off and near draw distance, as the first-launch check applies it).
- **"A tornado on screen"** is one tornado at a time, kept going: a tornado lives 45–70 game minutes (about 2 s at 3×), so the benchmark sends another across the city whenever none is left.
- **The 12 ms budget is the average frame time** in the heavy view at High; the 95th percentile is logged beside it. With the cap off and the GPU the bottleneck, the worst frames are the main thread waiting for the GPU queue, which a capped game doesn't build up.
- **The baseline was over budget** (heavy view 23 ms) before phase 3 changed anything, so M25 fixes it: tree regions rebuild only where the sim changed tree cells and at most once a frame, `civicAt` uses a spatial hash, and instanced meshes (cars, walkers, vehicles, trains, buses, planes, barriers) upload only the instances in use. Graphics only: no sim change.
- **What `models:check` accepts as a footprint.** The whole model exactly the lot or site and centred (135 of the first 136 are), or, for a zoned building, a half or a third of the lot's width (a row). The tenement is neither as drawn (16 m deep on a 24 m lot, with a cornice, steps and an awning past its walls), and SPEC-3 says to start with it, so two allowances, each reported as a note: a zoned model may stop whole cells short of the back of its lot (the game stands it at the front and leaves a yard), and a wall-to-wall building's footprint is its walls, with trim and steps overhanging up to 1.6 m at the front and 0.75 m elsewhere. Civic sites and annexes get neither.
- **Which way a model faces** is judged from what marks a front: entrances, shopfronts and canopies count most, signs and steps less, garage doors least; each looks away from the wall it is set in. A model fails when the back outweighs the front by half again (a back door or a rear garage doesn't make a front). On the 128 delivered buildings: none fails as delivered, and 114 fail when turned half round (the rest are symmetrical or have entrances both ways) (`scripts/dev/modelturn.ts`). Annexes are wings and may face any way.
- **The triangle budget** is the one in the model's own prompt; `R103.glb`, whose prompt makes a different design, takes its lot's (1,800). A budget is a limit, not a target: one triangle over fails.
- **Models are converted once, at build time, into one packed file** the game fetches (`assets/models-<hash>.bin`): 16-bit vertices, welded, with a role, flags, window unit and driven group per triangle; normals are rebuilt from the winding (flat, as the generator's are), which the reader has already made agree with the file's normals. Faces that can't be seen are dropped in the conversion (a wall's foot on its lawn slab, a window frame's back inside the wall: 18 % of the triangles delivered).
- **Two distant versions, not one**, both picked from the named parts: *far* drops sills and fittings under half a metre and turns panes and frames into flat panels (about 60 % of the near triangles, after the review kept the undersides that cast shadows); the *skyline*, for the whole-city view, keeps shapes over about 2 m and every pane (a quarter of near, and fewer triangles than the generator's towers). One far version alone would have made the whole-city view heavier than before.
- **What glows at night.** Windows and window bands light at random per copy; shopfronts and lobbies nearly always; big glazed volumes (a glass tower, an atrium, a conservatory, skylights) with a soft steady glow; balcony glass, solar panels, heliostats and grilles stay dark.
- **Where the levels hand over.** Near to far between 170 and 230 m, far to skyline between 520 and 680 m (scaled down on a low-resolution screen, where detail is lost sooner), each building fading across the band by a per-pixel dither that the next level complements, so there is no frame on which a building pops. Chunk meshes are 128 m near and far and 512 m for the skyline; a chunk wholly inside one level is drawn with a material that never discards (a discard costs this GPU its hidden-surface removal). Shadows hand over at a line in the middle of the band, not a dither: a dithered shadow map showed as hatching.
- **Hand-made and generated together.** A type with fewer than three designs shares its lots with the generator: with n designs, a look in n + 1 is generated (SPEC-3: "mix with the generator while fewer than three designs"); and no two neighbours of a type and size wear the same look.
- **A model stands only where it fits**: a lot as wide as it (or two or three times, as a row) and no shallower. Most level-2 and -3 buildings stand on the smaller lot they started on, so they stay generated (17 % of the bench city wears hand-made models). Squeezing a 24 m model onto a 16 m lot would break the toy-town scale; whether a smaller sibling's model may stand in is left to M28.
- **Civic signs and awnings keep the model's colours** (a fire station's red, a clinic's green cross); walls and roofs are repainted from the civic palettes as the generator's were.
- **Annexes.** A hand-made annex goes with a hand-made building, in the clear spot nearest the back corner the generator would use (nothing of the model over 2.2 m, shrunk step by step to 45 % if it must); a generated building keeps its generated annexes, which were drawn for its corners.
- **"A wall hangs in the air"** is a check the spec doesn't list: a wall part 6 m or longer held at one end, with 3 m or more and 40 % of its length over nothing (a gable slid off its building in three deliveries). Bridges, arches and canopies, held at both ends or by a part that butts them, pass.
- **A project being built** is its finished model cut off at the height the stage has reached, walls drawn from inside too, on bare earth inside the generator's hoarding, with the cranes and cabins in the nearest clear spot to where the generator stands them.
- **Seasonal lawns and hedges** on models (and now on generated lots) use the terrain's own season colours, so a garden turns with the ground round it; an open sheet in a double-sided material (a wave roof) is drawn from both sides.

### M26 Light and sky
- **Neutral tone mapping, not ACES.** Under ACES brick and other dark colours went near black in shade and the noon city read pale; Neutral keeps a toy's colours true. AgX was tried and looked washed out. Daytime exposure is 1.1, a little more while the sun is low, so golden hour glows rather than dims.
- **Only through the target when an effect needs it.** Occlusion needs the depth, so close up the frame is drawn into the multisampled target; at night from far off only the glow is on, laid over a copy of the finished screen; otherwise (Low always) the frame goes straight to the screen, which looks the same because the materials tone-map in every path. Low is then faster than at the start of the phase everywhere.
- **Shadow maps from far off at a lower rate**: drawn every frame within 600 m, 60 times a second to 1.5 km, 30 beyond. From far off they hardly move between frames, and redrawing them was most of the whole-city view's cost (about 4 ms at High).
- **At night a lit window is brighter from far off** (up to 2.2×), and the glow stronger, so the whole-city view twinkles rather than going dim under Neutral tone mapping.
- **Smoke and disaster dust take the light of the hour**: they were drawn at a fixed grey and read as glowing clouds at night.
- **Medium and Low against the start of the phase**: Low is faster in every view; Medium is faster in the heaviest view and the whole-city views, and up to about half a millisecond slower close up at street level, where its new occlusion (4 taps, 2× multisampling while on) and the night glow are on.
- **The frame is display-referred, 8 bits.** Rather than a floating-point scene target (two full-resolution resolves at High cost about 3 ms), three.js tone-maps into an 8-bit target as it does the screen (the target is marked as an XR target); the canvas has no multisampling, so every quality draws through the one target. The look with the effects off is the look before M26.
- **Ambient occlusion from depth only, at half resolution**, a warm violet in the corners (darker, never greyer); 12 taps and a depth-aware blur at High, 4 taps at Medium, off at Low. It fades out between about 380 and 900 m: from the whole-city view it would be a sub-pixel speck and costs nothing there.
- **Glow at night** for lit windows and lamps: a soft-knee bright pass in display space at a quarter resolution; High and Medium, off at Low.
- **Shadows fitted to the ground in view**, in two cascades at High (one map at Medium and Low): crisper near the camera than before (the old single map covered ±140 m at street level and left the rest unshadowed) and reaching 3.2 camera distances. The whole-city view uses one map.
- **The shadow pass draws only what casts**: panes, fittings close up and anything flat on the ground are left out (their shadows are the wall's, a pixel, or nothing); the overview's shadow triangles fell from 872k to 454k. Small things cast only in the near cascade.
- **Bounce light without a light**: a patch to three's lighting adds sunlight bounced from opposite the sun, tinted by the hemisphere light's ground colour (green in summer, gold in autumn, pale under snow), so no material needs a uniform of its own.
- **Golden and blue hours**: new keyframes, a glow round a low sun on the horizon, and the light turning from sun to moon across the blue hours (it used to jump at sunset).

### M27 Ground, lots and streets
- **Fields are farms turned about their own middles**, in staggered rows: turning the whole map's coordinates by a slowly varying angle bent the rows into arcs and diamonds far from the origin. A farm's edge is left as rough grass, and three farms in ten are grazing.
- **Paths a metre wide everywhere**: a fixed band of noise values widens into broad loops where the noise is flat, so the band is divided by the noise's slope (taken from the same four corners as the value).
- **Broad noise from a texture, not sine hashes**: four smooth noises per lookup in a 256² tiling texture. The first version (about 20 sine-hashed noise lookups a pixel) cost ~3 ms at High from far off; this one about 0.4–0.9 ms with the fields and paths on.
- **Ground detail is Low's cost switch**: fields, hedgerows, paths and the street furniture are off at Low (tone patches and mottle stay); hedgerows and paths are left out wherever a pixel spans more than about 2 m, where they'd only shimmer.
- **Tidy grass by the town's roads only**: motorways and railways cross open country without taming it (a band of town grass followed the regional highway).
- **There is no zone data map**, so the full zone look shows only while a zoning tool is out (any zone or dezone); data maps hide zone paint as before.
- **The subtle zone look is an outline where the zoned area ends plus a faint tint**: an outline alone lost the zone's colour at city zoom, a tint alone read as a stain. Built cells are left out, so the outline steps round finished lots.
- **Busy junctions**, for zebra crossings: three or more town roads where an avenue or boulevard meets, or a crossroads beside a commercial block. Decided by road types and zoning, not live traffic, so crossings don't come and go. Other junctions paint a give-way line on a side road narrower than the road it joins, and nothing between equals.
- **Kerbs were already modelled** (a 14 cm step) but painted the pavement's colour: the face is now darker and the stones along it a brighter, cooler grey, which is what makes the step read. (Cream stones read as lit windows to the M25 test's warm-pixel count by day, and real kerbs are grey concrete anyway.)
- **Visible cars at a level crossing, two fixes found by the longer frames** (Phase 2 review code, render only): a car entering a crossing now needs time to clear it counted from the end of its step, not its start (a long frame carried a car past the line after the train's time was read), and the count of cars already committed ahead skips cars that moved on to another road earlier in the same step (one that had finished its trip threw an error). The crossings spec failed 2 runs in 5 before, none in 8 after.
- **Road ribbons step 6 m on straight runs** (2.5 m round bends and always on bridges): the new kerb, edging and median strips would have nearly doubled road triangles; with the longer steps the overview draws 417k road triangles against 526k before, and roads over the hill town still sit on the ground.
- **Street furniture by frontage zoning**, not by what's built, so it doesn't rebuild as buildings come and go: benches and planters outside shops, bins outside homes, nothing by industry. Merged per chunk rather than instanced, so whole chunks are culled out of view and put away from 900 m.
- **Yards only behind hand-made models**: the generator's buildings already fill their lots (houses had gardens, paths and drives; markets their car parks), so the new surfaces go where a hand-made model leaves the back of its lot bare. Cars, markings and crates only in the near level.
- **Medians and verges follow the seasons and the snow on the fields** (they stayed summer green under snow); the per-road slush still covers asphalt and pavements only.

### M28 Buildings and variety
- **Generated distant versions from the same triangles, by marking detail**, rather than a second generator for far off: the near, far and skyline versions can't drift apart, and every generated building (zoned, civic, rubble, projects) fades through the levels as hand-made ones do.
- **Windows stay in the far version**: they are 60–96 % of a generated building's triangles and what a building is from the middle distance (and at night). Far drops fine parts only (about 1–33 % by type); the skyline also merges runs of alike windows along a band (offices, industry, luxury flats: 68–80 % of near), not separate windows (flats and homes: 88–98 %), where merging would turn dots into stripes.
- **Runs of alike windows, not one strip a floor**: a strip at the floor's average light read as a glowing pillar at night from far off.
- **Skyline chunks stay 512 m**: 256 m halved the city view's building triangles (buildings past 520 m are drawn in both the far and skyline levels while their chunk spans the hand-over) but added draw calls, and the heavy view got slower (10.8 ms against 8.8). On this GPU draw calls cost more than triangles.
- **The type in the generator's seed**: medium- and high-wealth offices (and other pairs of types) on the same lot with the same look drew the identical building, side by side in a city.
- **Residential towers by wealth**: the bench city's well-off towers came out nearly all white. Pastels with a fifth brick for the less well off, white or pastel with a few brick for the middle, white or modern greys and three in ten glass for the rich. Brick at a quarter or more read too dark for the bright, warm style.
- **No smaller sibling's model stands in on a lot its own type's model doesn't fit** (the question M25 left): a level's building keeps its own height and size, as its capacity and the skyline expect, and the refreshed generator carries the variety there. The hand-made share stays where M25 left it (17 % of the bench city, 25 % of the demo town); 8 m wide family houses and villas would let the low-density models appear (PROGRESS, "Models").
- **"No identical neighbours" checked as touching buildings drawn with the very same model** (`getNeighbours`): none in the demo town's 1,308 touching pairs. Two looks of a type can still be alike in colour; the neighbour rule and the wider palettes make that rare.
- **Medium and Low close up, a little slower than at the start, left so**: Medium's street level 0.7 ms by day and 0.85 at night, Low 0.1–0.35 ms (alternating runs). Medium's shadow maps close up were cut to 60 redraws a second (0.4 ms back); going to 30 would make moving cars' shadows trail, and dropping its occlusion saves only 0.1 ms. Every Medium view stays under 5 ms and every Low view under 3 ms, and both are well ahead of the start in the whole-city and heavy views, where weaker machines struggle most.
- **The skyline versions keep parts 2 m or more across that carry colour** (balcony bands, awnings, signs, rooftop plant, yards, containers, pools), as M25's rule keeps shapes over about 2 m: dropping them changed 2.8 % of pixels at the far-to-skyline hand-over in the M25 spec's view (the limit is 2.5 %); now 1.9 %.

Every view is now shot at an hour before the next hour.

### Model batch 2 (after phase 3)
- **A zoned model may be any whole number of cells up to its type's own lot, in width and depth** (as well as a half or a third of the lot's width, for rows), because buildings stand on smaller lots than their type's when the cells beside them were taken (the owner's second batch, `docs/models/PROMPTS-2.md`, is sized for those lots: a 16 × 24 m tower for the 16 m lots most R213s stand on). Placement is unchanged: a design stands on a lot as wide as it, or two or three abreast, and no shallower, at the front with a yard behind. The check names the lots such a model is for. Every model accepted before keeps its footprint.
- **Budgets from every batch of prompts** (`PROMPTS.md`, `PROMPTS-2.md`, … in batch order, not name order); a later batch's prompt for the same file wins (the remade C213).
- **Two designs or more on a lot: every look is hand-made**, the designs in turn, each repainted look by look and then mirrored (M25 mixed in the generator below three designs; the owner asked for two). One design still alternates with the generator: on its own it would make a street of one house in different colours.
- **No look-alike neighbours, by how buildings appear**: the look picker compares the design, each copy's paint and its mirroring with every touching neighbour's, of any type and lot size, so the same design painted the same way can't stand beside itself on a lot of another depth (an 8 × 16 villa next to an 8 × 24 one). `getNeighbours` counts those as identical too.
- **A canopy over garage doors is a loading dock's**, not an entrance's: it adds nothing to which way a model faces (its doors already count). The second factories (`I101-2`, `I111-2`) have docks down one side under a canopy, which outweighed their front door and failed them as "facing along X".
- **Garage doors weigh half what they did**: a twelfth of an entrance per square metre (0.25 against 3; was a sixth). A 4 m loading door at the back of a factory weighed as much as its front door, so the factories turned half round weren't caught. With the change no model fails as delivered (the most lopsided is 1.0 of the 1.5 that fails), and every model caught turned before is still caught: 155 of 168 turned half round (154 before; the rest symmetrical or with entrances both ways) and 160 of 168 a quarter round (159).
- **A model's own ground is lifted clear of the lot base**: the second batch lays its lawns at 1 cm and its paths at 4 cm (the spec only said "just above 0"), under and level with the 4 cm lot base the game draws under every building, so drives and paths flickered or vanished. The conversion lifts all of a model's ground by the same amount until the lowest lies at 5 cm (`GROUND_FLOOR`), and the check says so in a note; the first batch (lawns at 5 cm) is untouched.
- **Mirrored every other look**, not once the wall colours have run out: with six or more colours a design was never mirrored in a city's twelve looks, so a street kept every garage on one side. With an even number of colours the order turns over each time they come round, so no two looks agree until every colour has been both ways (as before).
- **Roofs and the other repainted parts turn with the look too**, from a place of the design's own, instead of at random: the walls' creams differ little, and the random roof often came back the same, so one design two lots along read as the same house. A second wall colour never repeats the first. Copies along a row still take neighbours' colours at random, never the copy beside them.
- **What the extra hand-made buildings cost**: in alternating runs against the build before batch 2, about 1 ms a frame at High at city and street zoom and in the heavy view (10.9 ms against 9.7, inside 12), 0.3–0.4 ms at Medium, nothing measurable at Low. The near versions are heavier than the generated buildings they replace (913 triangles a building against about 400–700) and cast more shadow; from the whole-city view the triangles hardly change (+1 %). Left as it is: the budget holds and the looks are what the batch is for.

### Model batch 3
- **The 19 batch-3 files came in as delivered**: every one passes the check as it stands (lawns 5 cm up, as spec v4 asks; no footprint, facing or budget trouble), so no check changed. `PROMPTS-4.md` is committed ahead of its models; it names only files that don't exist yet, so no current model's budget changes.
- **Long windows light in runs**, cut at conversion rather than asked of the owner: a lit window or band longer than 4.5 m along its wall becomes runs of about 3.5 m (`max(2, round(length / 3.5))` of them), each a window unit of its own, so at night a floor-long band reads as windows instead of one stripe. The way a window runs is square to the normal its faces share most area along, so a skylight or a saw-tooth roof's glass is cut along its length as a wall band is. Only the faces it looks out of are cut; a band's thin top and bottom faces that run past one run join the unlit strip (cheaper than cutting them, and they read as a sill). Pieces of one plane in one run are joined back into rectangles, so a band in two runs is four triangles, not six; the corners are put on a micrometre grid before joining (rounding noise along a run's edge had ordered them wrongly and dropped a corner, leaving 3-triangle runs). Daytime looks don't change: it is the same glass.
- **An unlit strip 0.4 m wide between two runs** (`UNIT_DARK`, never lit), so two lit neighbours still read as two windows. 0.2 m read as a dotted crack at city zoom; 0.4 m is about the spec's own 0.3 m floor for what reads. The strip is in the near and far versions (far is drawn from 170–230 m, where it is still about 3 px at 2×) and left out of the skyline (past 520–680 m, a pixel or less), where the runs meet halfway across it; the skyline keeps the runs, so a window doesn't change from lit to dark at the hand-over.
- **One band, one way**: a window's run direction is taken once for the whole unit, so a band made as one mesh round a corner (or a ring round a tower) would be cut along its main side only, and its other sides would light whole, each with one run. No model has one (every long window and band in the 195 is a straight strip facing one way, as the spec asks); `windowruns.ts` would list such a band as still long, and the fix then would be to plan runs per facing.
- **Shopfronts and glazed volumes stay whole**: shop glass is lit nine nights in ten (a long lit shopfront is what a shopping street looks like), and big glazed volumes glow softly, not window by window. 58 shop and lobby units over 4.5 m remain.
- **What the runs cost**: across all 195 models +8 % triangles near, +12 % far, +9 % skyline; in the bench city +114k near and far and +56k skyline across all its buildings; the models file grows from 3.3 to 3.7 MB (1.2 to 1.5 MB gzipped). Most of the batch's frame cost is its new designs: 417 of the bench city's buildings that were generated now wear one (the skyline towers among them were about 1,150 triangles generated; the new towers are 1,900–2,500 near and 900–1,560 skyline, every separate window a pane): heavy view at High 10.8 ms against 10.5 before the batch (12 allowed), city and street zoom 0.6–0.9 ms slower at High, 0.2–0.3 ms at Medium, Low the same. Left as it is: the budget holds, and separate windows that light one by one are what spec v4 asked for.
- **Lint ignores `dist-*`**: builds kept for before/after comparisons (`dist-before`, `dist-batch1`, already in `.gitignore`) made `npm run lint` fail on any machine that had them.

### Model batch 4
- **Three files failed because of the check, so the check changed, not the models** (the owner's call: the models are right). A wall is held up by anything under it reaching its foot, by overlap along and across it, rather than at 16 points along it and three across: `I113-5`'s conveyor stands on two legs and the second fell between two points, and `I203-3`'s open shed rests its gable on columns at its corners, outside the points across. It is only more lenient where something really is under the wall (2 cm of overlap or more), so a gable slid off its building still fails (the school's, from the first batch, still hangs 9 m).
- **Doors that face each other cancel out**: `I213-4`'s business units face each other across a lane down the middle of the lot, and their 16 doors counted as a front along X. Sideways evidence is now what looks out from a side wall (counted, as before) plus what looks in towards the middle from one side more than the other (pairs facing each other cancel). Cancelling every sideways pair would have let models with doors front and back pass when turned a quarter round (the catch fell from 265 to 256 of 291 in a trial); this way it stays at 265, and only parks and courtyards with doors facing in (`I203`, `I213`, `I223`, their second designs, the university) count differently.
- **Faces wound inside out are turned round at conversion**, as batch 2's sunken lawns were lifted: ten of batch 4's models (`C202-4`, `C203-4`, `C212-3`, `C212-4`, `I112-4`, `R122-3`, `R202-4`, `R212-4`, `R222-2`, `R223-4`) have a parapet round an L-, plus- or Y-shaped roof made as one outline with a hole, with the inside of the ring wound the wrong way, so the game (which draws one side of a triangle) dropped the far side of the parapet and it showed as a thin rim with the roof behind it (found in the review). A closed part whose faces disagree with their neighbours (an edge run the same way by both its faces) has them turned to agree, then the whole piece turned to look out if it encloses less than nothing (`orientFaces`); a part whose faces agree, or that isn't closed, is left as it is, so the other 289 models convert to the very same bytes. The check says which parts in a note. Asking the owner to remake ten parapets would leave them broken until then; the fix is exact.
- **The models file packed five times tighter instead of loaded later or in parts.** With batch 4 it grew from 1.3 to 2.0 MB as Pages gzips it, and in a fresh Chrome on a throttled line (`scripts/dev/startup.mjs`) the menu opened 0.3 s later at 20 Mbit/s, 0.6 s at 10 and 1.2 s at 5, and at 3 Mbit/s the menu's download missed `loadModels`' 6 s deadline in every run, leaving every building generated (a new city, with no demo town downloading alongside, made it with 0.15 s to spare). Loading part of it later would mean buildings changing their looks after the city has opened, and parts fetched side by side don't help a slow line. Instead the file (version 3) stores vertices and indices as differences from the one before (x's, y's and z's in runs of their own) and every model's arrays of one kind together, so a box's corners and a run of faces are small, repeating numbers: 457 KB gzipped against 2,038 KB, smaller than before the batch (1,317 KB), at the same raw size and the same 16-bit precision. Decoding is a running sum, 4–8 ms. ## Playthrough fixes (PLAYTHROUGH-FIXES.md)
- **P7: grading works in double precision.** Sample positions along a road, the smoothed ground and the bands a profile must stay in to meet its pinned ends are `Float64Array`s (heights stay single precision, as stored). In single precision the last sample could land a hair past the road's end, the far pin's band was empty there, no profile fitted and the road was planned at height 0: on flat ground at 3 m a 62.7 m street joining two dead ends was quoted $1,408 of earthworks (the playthrough saw $12–16k), and at 10 m some were refused as "No room to pass under the viaduct". Clamping the last sample would have fixed the one symptom; double precision removes the class. `fit` now says when it finds no profile and the road is refused ("Can't find an even grade for this road here") rather than planned at 0; no case in the sweep reaches it. Old saves keep the earthworks they made (terrain deltas), so there is no migration.
